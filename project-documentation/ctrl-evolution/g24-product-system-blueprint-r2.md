## The amendment in one minute

Thirty days is the current intensive proof window, not a technical expiry date.

CTRL should feel magical because it arrives prepared, recognises what is already known, asks only what remains worth asking and changes the work immediately when the person responds. The machinery can be exhaustive. The visible moment cannot be.

For every important uncertainty, CTRL chooses the lowest-burden truthful route:

```text
already known?
    |
    +-- yes -> use the accepted, current, permitted Brain item
    |
    +-- no -> obtainable from authorised work or reliable public evidence?
                 |
                 +-- yes -> enrich quietly, preserve provenance, do not ask
                 |
                 +-- no -> one person can answer cleanly in one moment?
                              |
                              +-- yes -> ask once, with the right control
                              |
                              +-- no -> would a prepared conversation change the decision?
                                           |
                                           +-- yes -> give Krish a specific session opportunity
                                           |
                                           +-- no -> hold the gap and abstain
```

This is not a better intake form. It is an evidence, interruption and conversation planner serving a consequential decision.

## The relationship is longer than a timer

The current Mindmake offer may begin with an intensive thirty-day proof. The product and data model must not stop working, purge context, force a release or imply completion on day thirty.

The engagement lifecycle is:

```text
preparing -> intensive proof -> continuing -> paused -> closing -> released
                                  ^             |
                                  +-------------+
```

`continuing` has no assumed end date. `ends_at` is optional. A review date asks whether another period of work has earned continuation; it is not an automatic product shutdown.

An engagement record needs:

- `started_at`;
- the configured intensive-window length and review date;
- current relationship state;
- the decision or value that justifies the next period;
- optional `ends_at` and close reason;
- source-specific permission and retention expiries;
- the latest mutually understood audience boundary;
- the next agreed human checkpoint; and
- release, pause, reopen and deletion receipts.

Permissions expire independently of the commercial relationship. Continuation does not silently renew a source consent, widen an audience or retain data beyond its authorised class.

The customer never sees a thirty-day course, countdown or completeness target. They experience a relationship that keeps returning useful judgement for as long as it earns that right.

## The intuitiveness contract

### What magic means here

Magic is not a generated image, an animated graph or clever copy. It is the felt combination of:

- **preparedness:** CTRL has already done the research and assembly it could do without the user;
- **recognition:** the moment is unmistakably about this person, company, category and decision;
- **restraint:** it leaves the user alone when nothing has earned interruption;
- **answerability:** the requested input is obvious and can be given in the natural form;
- **consequence:** the person can immediately see what their answer changed;
- **continuity:** later work begins from the right prior judgement instead of starting cold; and
- **control:** the person can correct, defer, decline, inspect or go deeper without learning the machinery.

The experience fails if it looks sophisticated while the user must translate an abstract diagnosis, administer their own profile or wonder what changed.

### Four questions the interface must answer

Every visible customer state answers one, and only one, of these first:

1. What matters now?
2. What do you need from me?
3. What changed because of me?
4. What does my Brain currently understand, and why?

Krish's operator room may combine these because his job is orchestration. The leader companion must choose the one that owns the moment.

### Visible choreography

```text
recognise the moment -> offer one useful move -> accept the natural response
        -> persist it immediately -> show the consequence -> offer depth on demand
```

The first frame contains one short orientation, one focal object and one primary action. It does not stack an announcement title, taxonomy label, explanatory subtitle, framework name, repeated custody language and progress copy around the same question.

For a leader question, the default anatomy is:

1. an optional one-line reason only when the reason is not already obvious;
2. the question itself;
3. controls that fully capture the required answer;
4. one primary action, plus a legitimate defer or decline path where needed; and
5. evidence, provenance and “why this matters” behind one deliberate reveal.

Optional notes stay optional. They cannot carry a missing name, number, reason or other value required by the selected answer.

### Interaction behaviour

- Mobile is one column and one-handed. Tap comes first, voice is available for a real incident or nuance, and typing is optional wherever possible.
- Input is locally preserved during network interruption and receives immediate visual acknowledgement. A failed save never clears the answer.
- Deep enrichment remains asynchronous. The last useful state stays visible; two loading ceremonies cannot appear back to back.
- Haptics, motion and sound may reinforce a transition, but none may carry meaning alone.
- The same action uses the same words and placement across the companion. Internal state names do not leak into customer copy.
- A user can always correct the current interpretation and see what the correction will affect.
- A quiet state is legitimate. CTRL does not manufacture an insight, question or score to keep the screen busy.

### Depth without clutter

The default layer shows the useful object. The next layer shows why it matters. A deeper layer shows evidence, standing, history and technical receipts. No layer repeats the previous layer in more words.

The Living Brain remains a visually impressive, inspectable expression of the product, but it is entered from a meaningful item or change. The map is never asked to substitute for an answer, a decision or evidence.

## The decision evidence map

Before CTRL researches or asks anything, it creates a coverage map for the live decision. This is not a profile-completeness chart. It is a list of decision variables that could materially change the call.

Each requirement records:

- the decision and exact variable;
- why that variable is load-bearing;
- what would change if it moved;
- its current standing and freshness;
- the best possible source class;
- who is actually capable of knowing it;
- sensitivity and audience ceiling;
- whether the answer may travel to later decision types;
- the lowest-burden acquisition path; and
- the stop rule for further collection.

The engine must distinguish:

- company fact from public company claim;
- a leader's public statement from their private judgement;
- stated preference from observed behaviour;
- recent behaviour from remembered generality;
- intention from committed action;
- importance from a real trade-off;
- a missing fact from an unknowable future; and
- a useful gap from curiosity about the person.

Only the first of each pair may be silently substituted for itself. Public specificity never authorises private personhood.

## The enrichment planner

### Objective

Fill the highest-value decision gaps with the least interruption and the strongest available evidence. Stop when the next unit of collection would not change the work enough to justify its cost, sensitivity or delay.

### Acquisition order

1. Current accepted Brain items with compatible audience, scope and freshness.
2. Material already supplied or produced inside the engagement, including transcripts, work, corrections, outcomes and final edits.
3. Authorised revealed behaviour, such as what was shipped, rejected, repeated, measured or rescued.
4. Primary public sources about the company, category and named public statements.
5. Reliable secondary sources where they add necessary context or contradiction.
6. Krish's direct observation, explicitly labelled as operator evidence rather than leader truth.
7. One leader interaction in the lowest-burden fitting format.
8. A prepared live session when the uncertainty is tacit, interdependent, sensitive or too consequential for a thin asynchronous answer.

The order is a default, not a blind pipeline. A sensitive private variable cannot be inferred from public material. An expiring decision may justify asking a bounded fact before a long research pass. Every deviation is recorded.

### External research scope

The public researcher may inspect, when relevant and current:

- official company pages, product documentation, pricing and strategic statements;
- filings, accounts and regulator records;
- newsrooms, investor materials and announced partnerships;
- job postings and public organisation signals;
- the leader's public talks, interviews, articles and attributable posts;
- customer, competitor and category moves;
- credible market, policy and technology evidence; and
- current news that could break an assumption behind the decision.

Social posts are statements, not proof of behaviour. Search-result summaries are discovery aids, not sources. Facts retain source spans, dates, audience, freshness rules and contradiction state.

### Efficient enrichment

- Resolve company and person identity before research; ambiguous identity fails closed.
- Reuse a public claim across authorised cases by reference, never by copying one customer's private reasoning into another Brain.
- Refresh by source version, content hash, freshness trigger or material event rather than researching every case from zero.
- Search to a named gap, not to an unbounded “tell me everything” brief.
- Stop retrieval when the decision variable is supported, contradicted, known to be private or honestly unknowable.
- Prepare research before the first session, before a booked session, when a material decision opens, when a watched assumption changes and before a prior Brain item is proposed for transfer.
- If external evidence answers the question, do not ask the leader to confirm a public fact merely to create engagement.

The enrichment result is a small set of supported facts, live tensions, contradictions, freshness risks and remaining questions. It is not a long dossier displayed to the customer.

## Question Intelligence

### Eligibility

A question may be asked only if the system can state in advance which of the following its answer can change:

- the framing of the current decision;
- which route remains viable;
- the evidence still required;
- a success threshold or kill condition;
- the human-versus-AI boundary;
- whether a prior judgement applies;
- whether a session is worth holding;
- the scope or standing of one learning proposal; or
- preparation for a named later class of consequential decision.

If it only makes the profile feel fuller, it is not earned.

### Question contract

Every candidate binds the following before delivery:

- `question_id` and `decision_id`;
- the missing variable;
- why it matters now;
- the evidence alternatives already checked;
- intended respondent;
- exact answer grammar;
- complete options or unit where applicable;
- what each answer changes;
- sensitivity, audience and retention;
- channel and expiry;
- follow-up condition;
- defer, unknown and refusal behaviour;
- stop rule; and
- the source or reasoning that caused the question to be proposed.

Question wording, controls and route effects are one versioned object. Changing one requires a new version of all three.

### Priority

Priority is based on a transparent rubric, not a model confidence number:

```text
decision consequence
+ uncertainty the answer can actually reduce
+ exclusivity of the respondent's knowledge
+ usefulness across future decisions
+ freshness and answerability
- cognitive burden
- sensitivity
- priming risk
- interruption cost
```

The rubric orders candidates. It does not pretend to calculate objective human value to a decimal point. Krish can inspect and override the rationale.

### Answer grammar

| Needed information | Default interaction | Guardrail |
|---|---|---|
| Correct or incorrect known interpretation | Confirm or correct | Show the exact interpretation; correction must replace the relevant value |
| One exclusive fact or route | Single select | Options are mutually exclusive and exhaustive, with “something else” only when it can be supplied |
| A real inventory | Multi-select | Use only when more than one answer can independently be true |
| Quantity or threshold | Numeric entry with unit | Do not hide the required denominator in an optional note |
| Recent behaviour | Bounded recall | Name the period and behaviour; do not ask for a vague self-rating |
| Degree or attitude | Five or seven fully labelled points | Avoid agree/disagree wording and unlabeled sliders |
| A direct trade-off | Forced choice | Make the sacrificed alternative explicit |
| Priority among more than five items | Repeated best and worst choice | Customer copy says “most” and “least”, not “MaxDiff” |
| Priority among five or fewer items | Short ranking | Never ask someone to rank a long mobile list |
| Tacit judgement or a revealing example | Recent critical incident, voice first | Ask for one concrete event before a general theory |
| Focused reason | One short open response | Ask after the choice only if the reason can change the work |

Every relevant interaction may provide `I do not know`, `Not enough information`, `Prefer not to say`, `Ask me later` or a scoped write-in when that is an honest possible state. Escape routes are specific, not a blanket option added without thought.

### Wording rules

- Use the person's vocabulary and name the real object, time and consequence.
- Ask one thing. Do not hide multiple decisions behind “and”.
- Do the abstraction inside CTRL. The leader supplies a choice, number, threshold, short fact or concrete incident.
- Capture the leader's provisional view before exposing CTRL's preferred route.
- Do not ask an attitude as yes or no when degree is what matters.
- Do not ask future intention when recent behaviour or a committed next step is available.
- Do not make a required answer depend on an optional note.
- Do not use consulting labels, internal framework names, faux intimacy or congratulatory value claims.
- The question must be understandable without its explanation. A fresh reader should be able to answer it without asking what CTRL means.

### Timing and delivery

Questions are triggered by decision need, not cadence or profile completion. Appropriate moments include:

- while opening a live consequential decision;
- after a new source creates a route-changing contradiction;
- before a booked session when one answer will materially improve the agenda;
- during a session when the next branch depends on the response;
- after a real outcome when the answer can distinguish luck, causation and transferable judgement; and
- before reusing a Brain item whose applicability is uncertain.

Only one question is visible at a time. The planner may prepare several, but later questions must be re-evaluated after every answer because the first answer may make them irrelevant.

The immediate result states what changed in plain language. If nothing changed, the question should not have been sent and becomes negative evidence for the planner.

## When Krish should run a live session

### Session opportunity rule

CTRL proposes a live session when a prepared conversation is likely to create more decision value than additional research or asynchronous questioning. Typical triggers are:

- a high-consequence decision with two or more interdependent gaps;
- conflict between credible public evidence and authorised internal evidence;
- tacit taste or judgement that can only be seen through examples and comparison;
- incentives, identity, politics or sensitivity likely to distort a thin written answer;
- a leader response that would change several commitments at once;
- repeated asynchronous ambiguity or contradiction;
- an artefact, live comparison or rehearsal that will reveal more than an abstract question; or
- a moment where Krish's challenge, synthesis or relationship judgement is itself the highest-value input.

The system does not propose a session for a public fact, one closed question, generic rapport, routine progress or work it can prepare itself.

### The prepared brief

Every session opportunity gives Krish:

1. the exact decision that needs to move;
2. one plain sentence on why a conversation is worth the leader's time now;
3. what the Brain currently sees, with standing and audience;
4. the two or three unresolved gaps that actually matter;
5. a short question ladder, beginning with a concrete event or fact, then a trade-off, then the pressure point;
6. the real options, artefact or contrasting examples to put in front of the leader;
7. the desired end state, including a call, threshold, experiment or evidence plan;
8. what should be captured afterwards and what must remain private; and
9. a suggested duration derived from the agenda, which Krish can change.

The agenda never asks Krish to conduct a generic discovery call. It should make the live time feel uncannily prepared.

### Current OS boundary

The authoritative Mindmake OS was re-read from `krishanraja/control-center` on 12 September 2026 and remains pull-only. Therefore the first implementation is a ranked `Session opportunity` in Krish's operator portfolio. It does not send an unsolicited Telegram, email or system push.

Krish can schedule, edit, snooze, dismiss or mark the opportunity unnecessary. Any future push channel requires a separate OS and product decision with its own interruption policy.

## Logical objects added by this extension

These are domain objects, not final table names:

| Object | Purpose |
|---|---|
| `engagement_period` | Records intensive, continuing, paused and closing periods without a hard day-thirty expiry |
| `decision_requirement` | Names one load-bearing variable and what it can change |
| `evidence_coverage` | Records what is supported, missing, stale, private or unknowable for that requirement |
| `enrichment_plan` | Chooses sources, order, budget, freshness and stop rule |
| `enrichment_receipt` | Preserves source attempts, results, failures and derivation |
| `question_plan` | Binds wording, answer grammar, timing and per-answer route effect |
| `answer_receipt` | Preserves the actual response, optional depth, audience and resulting change |
| `session_opportunity` | Gives Krish a decision-specific reason and prepared agenda for live work |
| `intervention_delivery` | Records approval, channel, expiry, delivery, deferral and suppression |

Models may propose these objects. Trusted application code validates and persists them. No model promotes a Brain item, widens an audience, sends a message or schedules a person.

## Extension to the first Crossing

The headless Crossing now begins with the decision evidence map and proves that the planner can choose not to ask.

The deterministic proof contains:

1. a high-external, low-internal case where public enrichment closes one gap and the leader is not interrupted;
2. a low-external, high-internal case where supplied work and observed choices are more useful than web research;
3. a high-external, high-internal case where one precise forced trade-off changes the route;
4. a low-external, low-internal case where CTRL abstains from diagnosis and prepares a focused live-session opportunity;
5. one answer that makes the next prepared question obsolete;
6. one optional voice critical incident that creates a provisional, permissioned learning proposal;
7. one correction that changes the coverage map, retires a question and repairs both decisions; and
8. one later materially different decision where the accepted judgement is proposed, challenged and either transferred or blocked.

Real public identities may be used only for public-source research tests, with no invented private material or simulated consent. Matched fictional profiles test controlled internal-evidence depth. Consented real material enters only at its later gate.

The customer UI is not built in this headless gate. The output is a readable receipt showing what CTRL knew, what it researched, why it asked or abstained, why a live session was or was not proposed and what each response changed.

## Internal evaluation

The extension adds **Question Yield** as an internal diagnostic, not a customer score or marketing claim.

A question yields value when its answer closes the named gap or changes the pre-registered route, evidence requirement, boundary, stop condition, session need or learning proposal. The engine also records:

- gaps filled without interruption;
- unnecessary-question rate;
- clarification requests;
- defer, unknown and refusal rate by question type;
- answer-control mismatch;
- questions made obsolete by a prior answer;
- research freshness and contradiction failures;
- session opportunities accepted, edited, dismissed and later judged useful;
- time to first specific value;
- interruption yield; and
- correction survival.

High Question Yield cannot excuse sensitive overreach, priming, false certainty or too many interruptions. Low question volume can be a sign of excellent enrichment rather than weak engagement.

## Experience proof requirements

After the headless intelligence proof, material UI concepts must diverge and be approved under the existing design process. They must then pass observed tests in the rendered medium.

The customer proof must show that a fresh participant can:

- identify why the current moment matters without an explanation from the facilitator;
- answer or defer without clarification;
- see what changed after responding;
- find deeper reasoning and evidence without it crowding the first view;
- recover from a failed save without losing input;
- distinguish a question, a Brain interpretation and their own decision;
- use the flow one-handed on a real phone; and
- understand the language at approximately a twelve-year-old reading level without making the underlying decision childish.

The operator proof must show that Krish can:

- see which customer and decision earned attention;
- inspect why research, a question or a session is recommended;
- change the channel, timing, wording or agenda;
- suppress a weak intervention;
- see what information is missing and who can know it;
- move from portfolio to prepared session without assembling context manually; and
- leave the customer quiet when that is the most intelligent action.

Cognitive interviews and actual-device tests are required before calling the interaction intuitive. Repository checks, synthetic fixtures and founder approval establish direction, not universal usability.
