## State of use

Krish is between customer work, often on desktop but sometimes steering remotely from mobile. He has a piece of text, a URL, a document, a voice thought or a Claude result. He wants to preserve it or use relevant Brain context immediately. He will not tolerate forms, technical vocabulary, unclear customer identity or ceremonial confirmation.

## User and action

The primary user is Krish as the operator of one customer's private Brain. He must be able to:

1. give CTRL new material with one deliberate action;
2. understand what was safely captured and what has not been assumed;
3. use a deliberately selected Brain slice in Claude;
4. bring resulting work back as a source without it becoming trusted memory.

The interaction must be understandable to a nontechnical twelve-year-old.

## Governing product and data rules

- The active customer and audience boundary remain visible before any capture or handoff.
- Ordinary paste is one deliberate action followed by an immediate receipt.
- New material defaults to private staging.
- CTRL asks only when ambiguity changes subject, audience, provenance or meaning.
- A Claude handoff includes only the smallest useful context for the named purpose.
- Operator-private material never appears in a customer-visible handoff.
- The whole Brain is never selected by default.
- The clipboard carries a small task starter, not private Brain contents, unless manual export is explicitly chosen.
- The first connector is read-only.
- A returned Claude result is AI-authored staged evidence, never trusted memory.
- Synthetic success demonstrates the interaction only. It proves no diagnostic quality or customer value.
- Failure preserves the original input and offers one plain recovery action.

## Required evidence in the proof

- Synthetic customer: Maya Chen, founder and CEO of Aperture House.
- Consequential decision: how Maya can make her quality standard usable by the team.
- Current supported read: the team misses early quality signals.
- Open question: what is the first warning sign Maya notices?
- At least three selected source references and one explicit exclusion.
- Capsule audience, freshness and expiry stated without security theatre.
- Return provenance distinguishes Maya's words, Krish's judgement, CTRL synthesis and Claude suggestion.

## Brand and interaction constraints

- Preserve the approved dark, warm, instrument-like Mindmake standard and the icon-only shell.
- Mint means current answer or safe completion. Amber means new or changed material.
- Use meaningful linework, motion or instrument marks, not generic sparkle icons, mascots, glass cards or decorative gradients.
- Keep wide-screen use no-scroll at 1440 by 900 where possible. Mobile may use progressive disclosure and natural page movement.
- One primary ask at a time. At least 44px controls. Keyboard and touch paths. Reduced-motion support.
- No nested scrollbars, huge announcement titles, motivational filler, grandiose claims or AI-written smart-arse copy.
- Plain human language in the visible surface. Hide MCP, capsule, schema, provenance IDs and token language unless deliberately inspecting details.
- Voice, file and paste paths must be apparent without making a toolbar feel like a connector marketplace.
- Every control must visibly work in the proof. Unimplemented controls must not be rendered as active.

## Required states

- ready to capture;
- pasted material received;
- ambiguity that genuinely needs one answer;
- duplicate source;
- processing delayed but source safe;
- ready to use in Claude;
- operator-private content excluded;
- copied task starter and Claude unavailable fallback;
- returned Claude result in staging;
- wrong-customer or expired-context failure that closes safely.
