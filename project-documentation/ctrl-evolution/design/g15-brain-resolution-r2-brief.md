# G15 Brain Resolution R2 visual comprehension brief

**Date:** 8 September 2026  
**State:** Authorised same-spine revision  
**Source of truth:** `g15-judgement-resolution-fixture.json`

## Outcome

Make one correction in Maya's Brain land as a picture before it lands as an explanation. A busy leader should understand the change instantly, feel its personal value and choose to inspect why.

## State of use

Maya opens the Brain on her phone between meetings. She gives the first screen five seconds. If the change feels true and useful, she may spend another twenty seconds on the evidence and next test.

## Governing rule

The visual may say only that a direct correction weakened the earlier delegation reading and replaced it with a narrower current reading. It must show that no held-out delegation decision exists. Proposed handoff routes are future test material, not historical evidence.

## First-frame story

Headline:

> Your Brain changed its mind.

Support:

> It thought you hated handing work over. You do not. You need people to see what good looks like first.

Limit:

> Still testing this on new work.

Primary action:

> Show me why

The headline, support and limit contain 29 prose words. The remaining opening budget is reserved for short labels inside the causal illustration.

## Causal illustration

The illustration uses simple objects and silhouettes, not a graph:

- **Old idea:** a work sheet moves from Maya towards the team, then loops back to Maya. Label: `Maya will not let go.`
- **Correction:** a visible edit mark cuts through the old loop. Label: `You corrected this.`
- **New idea:** the work sheet moves from Maya through a `What good looks like` reference card to the team. Label: `The team can carry the work when the bar is clear.`

The route changes rather than filling up. Amber owns the corrected old path. Mint owns the current supported path. Position and colour are repeated in text for non-visual comprehension.

## Evidence reveal

`Show me why` reveals two short visual evidence tiles:

- `Too many options came back for Maya to rescue.`
- `Maya wants the team to notice problems before she fixes them.`

One `Read her exact words` action reveals the complete canonical `SRC-101` and `SRC-105` quotations plus the `COR-02` wording. The default path does not require reading them.

The evidence limit stays beside the action:

> We have not tested this on a new delegation decision yet.

## Next contrast

The user compares two illustrated synthetic handoff routes:

- **A, find the route together:** feeling plus audience, team drafts a storyboard, Maya reviews early;
- **B, make the bar visible:** one good example plus one bad example, warning signs, team completes the first draft; and
- **Neither is ready.**

The question is:

> Which could work without you?

The choice remains local and provisional. It cannot change the Brain or territory state.

## Visual system

- Preserve deep ink, warm cream, mint and amber.
- Move from a long paper receipt to a bold split canvas with a compact receipt stamp.
- Use custom line-and-shape SVG illustration sized as a main product object.
- Use serif only for the human claim. Use sans for explanations and mono for controls and evidence IDs.
- Avoid equal card grids. The old and new routes form one story with a visible correction seam.
- Motion traces the old route back, then changes to the new route after the user acts. Reduced motion shows the final states immediately.
- On mobile, the illustration appears directly after the headline and before supporting text.
- On desktop, the claim and the illustration share the opening viewport at roughly two-fifths and three-fifths.
