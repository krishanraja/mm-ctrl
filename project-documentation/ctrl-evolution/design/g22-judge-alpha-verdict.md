# G22 judge alpha verdict

**Date:** 11 September 2026

**Role:** Independent primary concept judge

**Evidence boundary:** G22 sanitized brief R1; Candidates A, B and C; G20 founder gates R1 to R4; G14 Private Brain Builder R2 gate; G15 Brain Resolution R2 gate. No G22 judge verdict was consulted.

## Call

Choose **Candidate A, The Consequence File**, as the synthesis spine.

It is the only candidate that makes the whole CTRL product read as one causal record: work enters, CTRL notices a consequential tension, Maya changes the route, the Decision Table earns its place, Maya owns the call, learning stays proposed until she resolves it, later work changes because of that resolution, and the governed Brain leaves as the same record. That is the product.

This is a conditional win, not implementation approval. A becomes fit only if the render removes every hint of a guided timeline and makes the Living Brain plainly inspectable inside the path. As written, no candidate is ready to implement unchanged.

## Why A wins

A gives the founder the shortest truthful answer to the product:

> CTRL keeps one consequential case alive. It learns only from Maya's owned decisions, then shows exactly how that learning changes the next piece of work.

That sentence explains the Brain, the decision, the human boundary and the compounding loop without listing features. B needs an explanation of its field. C explains the intervention beautifully but not the whole product.

A also handles the most dangerous product rule correctly: a correction does not merely update a profile. It identifies affected work, marks it stale, preserves the earlier version and repairs only what depended on the rejected or corrected belief. This is a meaningful reason for the Brain to be living.

## Non-negotiable strengths to keep from A

1. **One case, one current edge, one changing work region.** There is no feature navigation and no second product hiding behind Brain inspection.
2. **Actions create state.** Future moments do not appear as locked stages. Quiet, sparse and stale cases are allowed to stop.
3. **The whole loop is visible through consequences.** Arrival, call, learning, later use, repair and release are parts of one governed record.
4. **The later-use proof is exact.** Before, because and now must point to one accepted learning version and one changed fragment.
5. **Correction is causal.** A correction preserves history, invalidates dependants and requires an explicit refresh.
6. **The Decision Table is earned and unchanged.** It is a deeper operator instrument, never home, and it is not squeezed onto Maya's phone.
7. **The data boundary is honest.** Synthetic evidence demonstrates mechanics only; source, standing and uncertainty affect the action at the claim that depends on them.
8. **Ownership ends the same record.** The portable copy is a release from the governed Brain, not a disconnected export feature.

## What A must borrow

### From C

- Borrow the one-note restraint. The current edge should initially show one prepared intervention, not an arrival summary followed by explanatory blocks.
- Borrow the discipline that companion intelligence is felt through preparation, restraint, specificity and silence. Do not name or personify a companion.
- Borrow the chip-led question structure and the exact later changed line. A user should recognise a concrete situation and tap, not decode `quality standard`, `travel` or `final rescue` in an abstract question.
- Borrow the empty quiet state. No empty card, prompt, badge or invitation should fill the absence of an earned interruption.

### From B

- Borrow the visible personal versus company ownership seam in Brain inspection and release.
- Borrow one plain ownership projection that answers what CTRL uses, who owns it, its standing and where it was used.
- Borrow the concrete scenario wording: if the pilot hits volume but still needs Maya's final rescue, has it worked?
- Borrow the rule that every relationship has a typed record and one plain-language meaning.

Do not borrow B's named field, four spatial positions, object taxonomy, source weave, decision boundary or multiple simultaneous traces. Those are explanation machinery.

## Veto conditions

Reject the A synthesis before implementation if any of these is true in a cold render:

1. The left rail reads as progress, onboarding or a five-step lesson.
2. Future moments appear before the events exist, or the user presses Next to advance.
3. `Arrival`, `Decision`, `Learning`, `Later return` and `Ownership` become large navigation labels. Use the real event and consequence, not programme language.
4. The first view needs more than one sentence to explain why CTRL interrupted Maya.
5. The route-changing question makes Maya interpret product language. It must describe a concrete outcome and offer plain choices.
6. The Brain is reduced to a source drawer, history list or release manifest. The path must expose what CTRL currently uses, what remains proposed, who owns it and what later work depends on it.
7. The case file looks like document management, a CRM timeline or a compliance log rather than live decision support.
8. The Decision Table becomes the default home, is redesigned, or is forced into a dense phone view.
9. More than one primary choice competes on any Maya screen, or desktop space is spent on a giant title, reassurance or internal status language.
10. The later preparation is unchanged after rejection, or a correction silently rewrites an earlier output.
11. A synthetic chain is presented as proof that CTRL improved Maya, the decision or company performance.
12. Personal and company material are merged for convenience in Brain inspection or release.
13. The representative path needs all eight actions because opening, inspecting or confirming was promoted into work. Remove every action that does not change the decision, standing or release.

## Why the others do not win

### B

B is the most ambitious visual concept and the least devastatingly simple. Its language is internally coherent but product-heavy: field, position, face, rail, weave, boundary, edge and trace. A leader should understand the decision, not learn this ontology. The concept also concentrates attention on a live Brain picture after the G14 gate explicitly made that picture subordinate to consequential work. Its strongest parts are components for A, not a governing spine.

### C

C has the strongest chance of winning the first five seconds. It may still be the better first rendered interaction inside A. It does not win the whole-product judgment because the margin is an intervention pattern, not yet the organising intelligence. Natural work arrival is assumed, Brain inspection and taking a copy sit outside the primary path, and the later proof arrives after release approval. That recreates the R4 failure in a subtler form: a polished local interaction stands in for the product.

## Fitness decision

**None is fit to implement unchanged. Candidate A is fit to synthesize and render.**

The next artifact should be one cold wide-desktop render of A's current case and one narrow-phone Maya question. It should use C's restraint and B's ownership seam. Do not render all five moments as a tour. The decisive test is whether a fresh viewer can say, without explanation:

1. what arrived;
2. why this call matters to Maya now;
3. what only Maya decided;
4. what CTRL was allowed to learn;
5. what changed later because of it; and
6. what Maya owns and can take away.

If the viewer instead describes a timeline, a set of features, a smart sidebar, a dashboard or an AI assistant, the concept has failed.
