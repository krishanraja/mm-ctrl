## Decision

**Winner: Sable.**

Sable gives the clearest first useful contrast, preserves the strongest distinction between record and inference, and has the most coherent path from concept to a calm mobile surface. Its editorial sequence is less participatory than Juniper's evidence placement or Quartz's lens, but it asks the least interpretive work of a leader who may be using the product inside a consequential conversation. It best satisfies the governing promise of devastating simplicity without converting simplicity into false certainty.

**Set-level hard-constraint verdict: PASS.** All three candidates satisfy the brief at concept level. None contains a disqualifying answer-first recommendation, named-person assessment, invented fact, technical administration, forbidden product metaphor or missing required recovery state.

**Set-level conceptual-distance verdict: PASS.** Every pair differs across all five named dimensions. This is genuine interaction divergence, not three skins on one flow.

### Hard-constraint residuals

- **Juniper:** the pass depends on the workbench remaining legible without a legend or spatial precision. If reflow turns it into an analytical dashboard or an unrelated fallback, it fails.

- **Sable:** the pass depends on source labels and first-reading recoverability surviving the final visual treatment. Without them, pacing would amplify inference into staged persuasion.

### Juniper scored reasoning

- **Clarity, 3.0:** “The team has not yet been tested against a clear new standard” is an excellent contrast. The preceding question, “What does this one-hour meeting actually bear on?”, is abstract and requires the leader to understand claim ledges before receiving value.
- **Agency and warmth, 3.5:** The leader contributes a real evidentiary judgment rather than merely approving CTRL's wording. Accept, resist, correct, irrelevant and unchanged-exit routes are excellent. The workbench language risks feeling clinical or like analyst work delegated back to the user.
- **Cognitive load, 2.5:** One ask does not automatically mean one easy ask. Three destinations, spatial meaning and a relationship judgment create the highest interpretation burden of the set, especially at 360 pixels, 200% zoom or during a live conversation.
- **Honesty, 4.0:** Unsupported territory becomes visibly empty, proximity has a deliberately narrow meaning, and the source partition is explicit. This is the strongest epistemic feature in the round.
- **State range, 4.0:** Sparse, slow, stale, conflicting, off-record, interrupted, corrected, irrelevant and zero-proposal states all follow directly from the governing evidence relationship.
- **Feasibility, 3.0:** The data model and owned-component boundary are credible. The risk is not engineering difficulty but conceptual survival when the spatial field collapses into normal flow for zoom and assistive paths.
- **Distinctiveness, 4.0:** Evidence behaving as load-bearing material is original and product-relevant. It is not novelty for decoration, provided observed users understand the field without explanation.
- **Grade: B+. Raise condition:** becomes an A-range candidate if unaided mobile testing shows that leaders understand the placement question immediately and the same evidence-gap meaning survives 200% reflow.

### Quartz scored reasoning

- **Clarity, 3.5:** Showing the evidence-qualified reading inside the same sentence makes comparison immediate. The full endpoint contrast is precise. The generic “Respond to this contrast” step adds a small layer between insight and judgment.
- **Agency and warmth, 3.5:** The gesture is reversible, tactile and non-coercive in mechanics. The user can resist and restore their own words. However, moving from “My view” toward “Evidence so far” subtly turns the rail into a path from subjective to true.
- **Cognitive load, 3.5:** One statement and one control are easy to scan. The gesture is simpler than Juniper's classification, though continuous positions may imply meaningful degrees that the data does not support.
- **Honesty, 3.0:** The provenance line is strong, but the interaction metaphor works against it. The right endpoint receives structural authority before the leader has judged the inference.
- **State range, 3.5:** All required states are covered and the correction reset is coherent. In sparse or slow states, the signature lens offers less value and risks becoming inactive chrome.
- **Feasibility, 4.0:** A semantic range with pointer, tap, keyboard and screen-reader paths is straightforward in the stated stack. The structure can reflow without depending on arbitrary model-authored layout.
- **Distinctiveness, 3.5:** The moving lens is memorable and materially linked to comparison. Its authority risk is intrinsic enough that it cannot be dismissed as a copy tweak.
- **Grade: B+. Raise condition:** becomes an A-range candidate if fresh testing shows no truth-endpoint interpretation and the continuous rail can be made categorical enough to avoid false precision.

### Sable scored reasoning

- **Clarity, 4.0:** The first reading, record passages and second reading create the most immediate mental model. The contrast itself cleanly separates visible hesitation, prior operating conditions and the untested adoption question.
- **Agency and warmth, 3.5:** The tone is patient and respectful. Pace, cancel, correction, resistance and irrelevance are all available. The leader is still more reader than co-investigator until the proof mark appears.
- **Cognitive load, 3.5:** Single-column natural flow is the safest fit for phone, zoom and keyboard states. Sequential replacement limits simultaneous clutter but creates some working-memory and pacing cost during a live conversation.
- **Honesty, 4.0:** Every transformation carries a source class, the original remains recoverable, the second reading is explicitly a reading rather than a finding, and the unknown remains specific.
- **State range, 4.0:** The temporal spine handles abstention, stale or conflicting context, interruption and correction without inventing a new interaction model for each case.
- **Feasibility, 4.0:** This is the smallest reliable state machine of the set. It relies on ordinary semantic controls and structured passages, and reduced motion preserves the complete meaning.
- **Distinctiveness, 3.5:** “Read twice like an editorial proof” is a coherent signature rather than a generic loading reveal. It is less novel than Juniper, but novelty is not the objective.
- **Grade: A-. Raise condition:** becomes an A if observed leaders feel they are thinking with CTRL rather than being presented to, and if the deliberate pacing reaches the useful contrast fast enough during live use.

## Why Sable wins

The decisive product problem is not merely to show different words. It is to let a leader understand why the evidence changes what can responsibly be claimed. Sable's sequence does this with the fewest new rules. “First reading” protects the leader's prior, the labelled record passages expose the basis of change, and “second reading” communicates interpretation without pretending it is fact or final authority.

Juniper is more ambitious as an instrument and Quartz is more tactile, but both ask the metaphor to carry epistemic meaning that may become fragile under mobile reflow or unaided interpretation. Sable's meaning survives if motion is removed, if the viewport narrows and if the user returns after interruption. That resilience matters more than having the most novel gesture.

## Strongest discarded feature

**Juniper's unsupported space.** Making the absent evidence perceptible as an intentional blank is the strongest single idea outside the winner. It turns abstention from a disclaimer into part of the object and makes “what this does not establish” as legible as the supporting record. A synthesis should preserve that principle without importing Juniper's claim-placement task or spatial dashboard risk.

## Material dissent

The main dissent against Sable is that it may produce a beautifully calibrated reading rather than a genuinely participatory act of judgment. Quartz gives the leader more immediate embodied control, and Juniper more directly exercises the calibrated-system-judgment capability that CTRL eventually wants to build. If observed users say Sable feels like CTRL is reading at them, or if the paced evidence sequence interrupts rather than supports a live conversation, that is a concept-level failure, not a motion-polish issue.

The strongest alternative is Quartz, not because its score is closest, but because it tests the opposite hypothesis: that reversible touch can make evidence qualification feel self-discovered. Its promotion condition is strict. Users must not describe the right endpoint as truth, proof or CTRL's answer. If they do, the lens mechanism should be killed.

Juniper should not be selected as the primary synthesis spine for this round. Its evidence judgment is intellectually strong but asks the busiest user to perform the most system-like work before receiving value. It remains the best source of one feature, not the best governing sequence.

## Next gate

Render Sable's first-contrast synthesis at `390 × 844` with Juniper's unsupported-space principle expressed without a placement task. Before implementation, test the render cold for three observable conditions:

1. The leader can state the contrast and the remaining unknown without explanation.
2. The leader describes the interaction as helping them think, not presenting a finding about the team.
3. The first useful contrast remains fast, complete and actionable at `360 × 800`, 200% zoom, reduced motion and with a virtual keyboard present.

Failure on condition 2 should reopen Quartz as the materially different alternative. Failure on conditions 1 or 3 should stop synthesis rather than add more passages or controls.
