## Decision

**Winner: Candidate Juniper.**

Juniper best converts the product thesis into an interaction. The leader must make one bounded judgment about what a piece of evidence actually bears on, then CTRL exposes the unsupported territory beside that action. This makes the useful contrast something the leader helps establish, not merely something the system presents. It is the strongest combination of human agency, causal sharpness, epistemic honesty and distinctiveness.

**Ranking:** 1. Juniper, 2. Sable, 3. Quartz.

Juniper's lead is real but narrow. Sable is the safer implementation and may prove better in the stated live, divided-attention use moment. Juniper should therefore advance only if the first render preserves a single obvious action without turning the field into a miniature dashboard or an evidence-classification exercise.

### Sable: 25 / 28

- **Clarity, 4:** The contrast is exact and causally useful: evidence gathered before committed strategy, incentives and success measures cannot isolate team adoption capability. The `First reading` / `Second reading` frame makes the change legible without changing the leader's words.
- **Agency and warmth, 3:** `Fair`, `Not convinced`, `Needs correction` and `Not relevant` preserve resistance, and the editorial tone is patient. However, the leader is mainly the audience for a paced interpretation until the response ask. CTRL performs more of the decisive action than the leader does.
- **Cognitive load, 3:** One changing reading position and sequential passages are calmer than a report. The cost is time and passivity: three evidence beats, a second reading, a four-line ledger and then a response can feel like a presentation inside a live conversation.
- **Honesty, 4:** This is the strongest explicit provenance treatment. The four source classes, lack of mint, stated unknown and withdrawal after correction all prevent polish from laundering inference into fact.
- **State-range coherence, 4:** Sparse evidence becomes an abstaining second reading; stale and conflicting context narrow or stop the reading; interruption resumes at the committed beat; correction withdraws and reruns the interpretation. Each state belongs to the metaphor.
- **Feasibility, 4:** The same-position text replacement, structured passages, semantic response controls and natural single-column layout map directly to existing primitives. The concept needs no novel interaction infrastructure.
- **Distinctiveness, 3:** Reading a decision twice is memorable and coherent, but the core interaction remains a highly crafted linear reveal. Its edge comes more from editorial sequencing than from a new human-AI reasoning behavior.

### Juniper: 26 / 28

- **Clarity, 4:** The ask, “What does this one-hour meeting actually bear on?”, makes the causal problem concrete before the contrast appears. The empty adoption area then carries the exact insight: current evidence has not tested the team under the changed conditions in the leader's own flip rule.
- **Agency and warmth, 4:** The leader contributes the key epistemic move, can place the evidence outside both claims, and can leave with their view unchanged. CTRL responds to the leader's judgment rather than staging itself as the wiser reader. The workbench metaphor feels collaborative and physical without pretending personhood.
- **Cognitive load, 3:** The task is bounded to one slip and three plain destinations, but “what does this bear on?” is still a small reasoning test. In a divided-attention conversation, some leaders may hesitate or feel examined. The score cannot reach 4 until an unprompted mobile test shows immediate comprehension.
- **Honesty, 4:** Unsupported territory is represented as absence, not weaker confidence. The concept explicitly separates the leader's claim, evidence, inference and unknown, and allows `This does not answer either`. This is the best mechanism-level expression of abstention in the set.
- **State-range coherence, 4:** Sparse evidence, slow analysis, stale/conflicting context, off-record use, interrupted return, correction and zero proposal all remain variations of whether a relationship is committed, excluded, withdrawn or absent. No state requires fake certainty to preserve the effect.
- **Feasibility, 3:** The structured relationship set and non-spatial fallback are credible. The remaining difficulty is material: at 390 pixels, 200% zoom and keyboard-open state, the field must remain an instrument rather than degrade into three ordinary buttons followed by a card. It is feasible, but carries more responsive and semantic design risk than Sable or Quartz.
- **Distinctiveness, 4:** The useful contrast is produced through evidential attribution and visible unsupported space. That is not novelty pasted onto a normal answer flow; the interaction embodies CTRL's distinction between what evidence supports and what remains unproved.

### Quartz: 23 / 28

- **Clarity, 3:** The before/after statement is concise and the provenance line is strong. The continuous rail adds an ambiguous middle, though: the decision is not genuinely a spectrum between “My view” and “Evidence so far,” so partial positions may imply degrees of truth that the data model does not support.
- **Agency and warmth, 3:** Reversibility is excellent, and returning to the leader's words communicates respect. Yet the most important gesture primarily reveals CTRL's prepared endpoint. The leader controls exposure more than interpretation.
- **Cognitive load, 3:** A single tactile control is simple and one-handed. It nevertheless asks the leader to learn a novel lens behavior, and the significance of intermediate positions may be unclear. The explicit instruction helps but also signals that the metaphor is not entirely self-evident.
- **Honesty, 3:** Provenance and unknowns are explicit, but the governing metaphor works against them. A lens conventionally reveals what is really there, and the labelled right endpoint can feel epistemically superior. The submission correctly identifies this as a mechanism-level risk.
- **State-range coherence, 4:** The lens has clear honest behavior for absence, slow work, stale or conflicting context, off-record capture, interruption, correction and zero proposal. Resetting after correction is especially coherent.
- **Feasibility, 4:** A clipped before/after text layer with a semantic range, binary tap alternatives, structured fields and owned markup is technically bounded. The accessibility fallback is well anticipated.
- **Distinctiveness, 3:** The physical lens is visually memorable, but the interaction risks becoming novelty theatre because continuous manipulation adds little decision value beyond comparing two fixed readings. Its best property is reversibility, not the slider itself.

## Strongest discarded feature

**Quartz's reversible same-place return to the leader's untouched words** is the strongest discarded feature. It makes disagreement and correction physically credible. If carried into the Juniper synthesis, preserve the principle as a simple binary `My view` / `Evidence so far` compare or immediate undo after placement. Do not import a continuous truth-axis slider, which would also import Quartz's main honesty risk.

## Material dissent

The strongest dissent from the winner is in Sable's favor.

Sable may outperform Juniper in the actual state of use because it demands less simultaneous interpretation. Its stable reading position, controlled pace and explicit source ledger are safer for a leader who is speaking with a customer and holding a phone in one hand. If cold testing shows that more than a small minority of leaders pause to decode the ledges, ask what “bears on” means, or feel that CTRL is testing their evidence literacy, Juniper's mechanism has failed at the product level and Sable should become the winning spine.

Quartz does not create equivalent dissent. It is the easiest signature object to remember, but its truth-revealer implication is too close to the exact oracle behavior the brief prohibits.

## Recommendation and next gate

Advance **Juniper** to one rendered mobile synthesis. Preserve its single evidence placement, meaningful unsupported space, one response ask and `Leave with my view unchanged` escape. Borrow Quartz's immediate reversible return to the original view as a binary affordance, and borrow Sable's explicit four-part source distinction only if it can remain available without adding a report below the field.

The render should be killed before founder review if it needs a legend, connecting lines, pan, zoom, multiple placements, explanatory onboarding, a fixed action dock that collides with the keyboard, or any visual treatment that makes proximity look like confidence. The acceptance signal is that an unaided leader at the target mobile viewport can explain, after one placement, both what the evidence supports and what remains unproved.
