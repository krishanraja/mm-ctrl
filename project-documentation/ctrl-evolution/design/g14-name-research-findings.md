# G14 product-name research findings

**Retrieved:** 7 September 2026

**Decision status:** founder-approved 7 September 2026. Mindy is removed from consideration. CTRL remains a working/system name only; final product naming is deferred to the pre-launch GTM gate.

## Executive judgement

The founder's diagnosis is strong: the experience should feel like a perceptive participant in the Mindmake thirty-day proof, not software the user operates. The proposed name is weak: **Mindy should not become the public product name on current evidence.**

The name carries warmth and conversational affordance, but it is materially crowded in the exact AI-assistant territory. More importantly, it risks misdescribing the product. A universal human-like female-coded assistant can make the customer's own Brain feel secondary, shift expectations from sovereign judgement to agreeable service, and imply a level of personhood, agency and reliability the system must instead earn through visible evidence and calibrated behaviour.

CTRL is not protected from challenge. It is terse, cold, generic, visually technical, awkward to say aloud, and semantically close to command-and-control. Those weaknesses are real and may justify a later rename. They do not make Mindy the right replacement.

**Approved architecture call:** retain Mindmake as the master brand and CTRL as the internal/system working name through the build. Preserve the *participant interaction model* without creating a Mindy identity. Let the customer's Living Brain remain the protagonist. Run a deliberate, ownability-aware naming decision at the pre-launch GTM gate, when the Private Brain Builder and Customer Brain behaviours are tangible enough to name honestly.

## Source-backed findings

### The name is already occupied in directly adjacent territory

- `mindy.com` describes Mindy as an email-first AI agent for complex workflows and context, later expanded into creator and brand-team content workflows. Its team has since joined WorkWhile, but the domain, history and recognition remain occupied: <https://mindy.com/>.
- A separate current French company, Mindy SAS, describes a platform for business leaders and executive teams with specialist AI assistants for strategy, finance, sales, marketing, HR, legal and operations; solo and group conversations; AI-facilitated meetings; decision projects; and private/company knowledge bases. This is not an incidental naming collision. It overlaps the proposed product's buyer, interaction model and decision territory: <https://mindy.thenextmind.ai/cgu>.
- Other current products use Mindy for an AI chatbot builder, a plain-language visual assistant, mental-wellbeing and neurodivergent companions, and planning support. This reinforces the name's existing association with a friendly generic helper rather than an ownable category: <https://mindy.ai/>, <https://www.showmindy.com/>, <https://web.mindy.kr/en>, <https://mindycore.com/en>.

This is a commercial collision screen, not trademark clearance. Official UK and US register searches and legal review remain required before any public naming decision.

CTRL is also commercially weak on ownability. Current uses include an AI-agent graph control plane, an AI-agent security proxy, a cybersecurity company and a large multichain wallet: <https://ctrlnode.ai/>, <https://getctrlai.com/>, <https://ctrl.co/>, <https://ctrl.xyz/>. These are less direct than the executive-decision Mindy collision, but they prevent “keep CTRL” from being mistaken for a clean naming clearance.

### Human-likeness can improve feeling more than behaviour

- A 2025 meta-analysis of experimental studies found human-like social cues had moderate effects on perception, small-to-moderate effects on rapport, trust and positive affect, but very small effects on behavioural outcomes. Effects were stronger in free-text interaction and tended to matter more in lower-stakes contexts: <https://www.nature.com/articles/s41599-025-05618-w>.
- An incentivised 2024 experiment found that people could over-rely on AI advice even when it contradicted contextual information and their own assessment, underlining the need for calibrated trust rather than maximum perceived trust: <https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0747563224002206>.
- A 2022 controlled study of gendered AI assistants notes that female names and voices paired with service roles can reproduce stereotypes of passive assistants. It also found user responses varied with gender and perceived agency rather than following one universal preference: <https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2022.855091/full>.

The evidence supports warm, natural, socially fluent interaction. It does **not** establish that a human name causes meaningful return, better disclosure or better decisions in this product's high-stakes setting.

## Strategic scorecard

These ratings are product-strategy inference, not research measurements.

| Criterion | CTRL only | Mindy full rename | System plus named participant |
|---|---|---|---|
| Immediate warmth and addressability | Low | High | High |
| Current same-category ownability | Low-medium | Very low | Depends on participant name |
| Enterprise and consequential-work gravity | High | Low-medium | High if roles are clear |
| Customer ownership of their Brain | High | Low-medium | High if the Brain remains protagonist |
| Infrastructure, export and platform stretch | High | Low | High |
| Risk of generic-assistant expectations | Low | Very high | Medium |
| Trust-calibration fit | High | Low-medium | High with explicit evidence/authority cues |
| Explanation burden | Low | Low | Medium-high |

### Strongest case for Mindy

“Mindy” is easy to say, invites direct address, creates a memorable person-in-the-room metaphor and naturally belongs beside Mindmake. In a thirty-day relationship, that could make contributions feel like conversation rather than data entry. “Mindy noticed something” is emotionally and linguistically easier than “CTRL generated an inference.” It may also make demonstrations and word of mouth more vivid.

### Why that case does not survive intact

1. **It borrows warmth before earning competence.** The name can increase social expectations immediately; the Brain's evidence, correction and judgement quality will mature gradually.
2. **It makes the wrong entity famous.** The durable asset is the customer's evolving Brain and judgement, not a universal synthetic character owned by Mindmake.
3. **It narrows the perceived job.** “Mindy” sounds like a helpful personal assistant or coach. The ambition is to help leaders redesign work, organisations and categories while preserving sovereign taste and accountability.
4. **It creates gendered role baggage.** A feminine-coded helper attached to capture, reminders and preparation can reproduce the exact service-assistant frame the product is trying to exceed.
5. **It is commercially crowded.** One active Mindy product already targets executives, decisions, meetings and knowledge bases. A suffix or “powered by CTRL” does not remove that confusion.
6. **It weakens portability semantics.** “Export your Mindy” sounds like transferring a proprietary agent; “export your Brain” or its governed package communicates that the asset belongs to the customer.

### Strongest case against CTRL

1. It can sound harsh, technical and emotionally sterile.
2. It is awkward in speech: users may say “control,” “C-T-R-L,” or “the CTRL app.”
3. The command/control meaning can contradict user sovereignty.
4. It is also crowded across AI, cybersecurity, crypto and open-source tools, so its current name is not inherently defensible.
5. A compressed startup-style name may date faster than the enduring human promise.

Retaining CTRL now is therefore a sequencing decision, not brand complacency.

## The sharper architecture

### Master brand

**Mindmake** owns the worldview, commercial relationship and thirty-day proof.

### Protagonist

**The customer's Living Brain** is what grows, surprises, becomes useful, travels and remains theirs.

### Participant behaviour

The product behaves like a brilliant, warm and challenging collaborator: it remembers, notices movement, asks the next valuable question, prepares the room, admits uncertainty, and shows what changed. It need not pretend to be a person to produce that feeling.

### System name

**CTRL remains provisional** for the governed infrastructure, product corpus and build until a naming exercise produces a candidate that is both emotionally apt and commercially ownable. It does not have to dominate customer-facing copy.

## Pre-launch naming test

Do not ask users which name they prefer in the abstract. Put the same three-minute experience in front of target leaders under three blinded treatments:

1. CTRL as product and speaking presence;
2. an ownability-screened warm name or named participant with the Brain still visibly owned by the customer;
3. no named participant: Mindmake plus “your Living Brain,” written with the same warmth and intelligence.

Measure:

- unaided recall after 24-48 hours;
- willingness to contribute one piece of meaningful context;
- perceived sophistication and enterprise credibility;
- whether the user understands who owns the Brain;
- calibrated trust: what they would verify rather than blindly accept;
- willingness to bring it into a consequential working session;
- the words they spontaneously use to describe the product.

**Selection rule:** adopt a new public name or named-participant architecture only if it materially improves meaningful contribution and delayed recall without lowering ownership comprehension, calibrated trust or consequential-work credibility. Every public candidate must first pass exact-category collision, domain and professional trademark screening. Mindy is excluded from the candidate set rather than held for later reconsideration.

## Unknowns

- No legal trademark clearance has been completed for CTRL or Mindy.
- No target-leader behaviour test has compared future ownable candidates or named-versus-unnamed interaction.
- The degree to which CTRL can be softened through verbal identity, typography and interaction has not been tested.
- The right long-term product name may only become obvious once the operator and customer expressions are both tangible.
