# G14 reset tiebreak verdict

**Date:** 8 September 2026
**Review mode:** Fresh blinded tiebreak
**Anonymous order reviewed:** Candidate D, Candidate H, Candidate P
**Authority:** Advisory selection for one rendered founder proof only
**Decision:** Candidate D should govern the next render

## Review boundary

This verdict uses only the reset brief, judge rubric, distance and feasibility record, synthesis record, R1 founder gate, R2 brief, R2 founder gate and the three anonymous candidate concepts named for this review. No earlier judge verdict was inspected.

Hard constraints were assessed before weighted criteria. A fixture or frame correction is treated as a binding pre-render guardrail only where the supplied feasibility record says it can be made without changing the candidate's sequencing, user agency, primary interaction, information structure or state model. No score overrides a hard-constraint failure.

## The call

Select **Candidate D, the Decision Bench**, as the governing spine for the next render.

Its central act is the closest match to the real job: compare current and historical meaning, inspect the exact evidence, see what uncertainty changes, then prepare one next move. It converts the wide desktop into simultaneous working regions rather than another announcement-led page. The current interpretation alone guides action, history remains inspectable but non-operative, and private exploration stays local to the working view.

Candidate D must not be rendered exactly to its stated pixel arithmetic. The available content width is 1320 px, not 1344 px. The corrected primary row is binding: **462 px comparison table, 24 px gap, 570 px evidence bench, 24 px gap and 240 px action dock**. This preserves the spine and fits the target frame.

### Candidate D: 88, B+

Candidate D wins because its information structure does the most consequential work at once. Current meaning, alternative or historical meaning, exact source rows, the effect of uncertainty and the next move remain mutually visible. The user can challenge the basis without losing the action consequence. It becomes an A if the first render proves that five rows and three regions remain immediately legible rather than becoming an analyst dashboard.

### Candidate H: 82, B

Candidate H has the strongest expression of the Brain as a living reasoning structure. Typed direction, provenance and historical non-operability are native to the primary interaction. It loses on immediate decision usefulness because the attention lens asks the operator to learn a spatial grammar and makes `next inspection` more central than preparing the consequential move. It becomes an A only if a fixture-truthful route can pass the ten-second comprehension test without explanation and without inventing edge uncertainty.

### Candidate P: 79, B-

Candidate P has strong one-screen discipline and a useful distinction between understanding a belief, testing it and considering a consequential commitment. It loses because the cockpit and clearance language create false precision where the fixture has no thresholds. Removing computed permission risks leaving the central lever as a view filter with less practical value than the Decision Bench. It becomes an A only if the control changes the evidence question in plain language while never implying that the system has authorised an action.

## Disguised repetition and invariant regression

### Candidate D

**Disguised repetition:** No. The rejected Intervention Lens relied on an intervention-first, announcement-led sequence with later sections for change, preparation and Brain inspection. Candidate D replaces that sequence with simultaneous comparison, evidence inspection and action consequence. Its state changes through selected and pinned cells, not through scrolling a prepared story.

**Invariant regression:** No product or data invariant is structurally regressed. The stated desktop width is wrong by 24 px, so the one-screen requirement would regress if the numbers were copied into the render. This is a frame specification defect, not a reason to abandon the spine. Its `Use next move` label also needs correction because the proof has no execution authority.

**Discarded strength to restore:** Restore the rejected work's concrete preparation usefulness: exact opening, material to bring, listening signals and fallback. Put this inside the existing action dock behind **Prepare next move**. Do not recreate an intervention hero, a tall preparation card or a later page section.

### Candidate H

**Disguised repetition:** No. The Brain terrain is the primary object and route traversal is the primary action. It does not reproduce the rejected editorial sequence. Its visual novelty is real, although it carries a separate risk of becoming an ornamental map.

**Invariant regression:** Yes, unless corrected before render. The concept treats the decision gap as an uncertain relationship, but the feasibility record says the fixture contains `GAP-014` as a separate knowledge gap and does not encode relationship-level uncertainty. Rendering an open uncertain edge would invent meaning. This is the candidate's material truth risk.

**Discarded strength to restore:** Restore the rejected work's direct session usefulness. Keep the current move visible at the fixed intervention anchor and show one compact preparation consequence in the section cut. Do not add a hero, a sequence of editorial cards or a separate preparation page.

### Candidate P

**Disguised repetition:** No, but it is the closest of the three. Its decision and answer opening, amber prior interpretation, evidence window and next move could collapse back into the rejected Intervention Lens if the lever does not materially change the working state. The render must prove that the control reorganises scrutiny, not merely highlight another summary.

**Invariant regression:** Yes, unless corrected before render. The fixture has no Understand, Test and Commit thresholds or clearance rules. Any `Supported`, `Constrained`, `permitted move` or automatic fallback to a lower level would claim decision authority the data does not contain. The candidate also names relationship types such as `contradicts`, `depends on` and `supersedes`; these cannot be rendered unless they are exact fixture values.

**Discarded strength to restore:** Restore the rejected work's causal chronology: latest evidence, earlier interpretation, current interpretation and future test. Express it as one compact change trace within the evidence instrument. Do not turn it into a headline or a vertically revealed story.

## Binding fixture and render guardrails

These are acceptance conditions for the next render.

1. **Correct the desktop grid before styling.** After the 72 px rail and two 24 px gutters, use exactly 1320 px: 462 px comparison table, 24 px gap, 570 px evidence bench, 24 px gap and 240 px action dock. No horizontal compression, hidden overflow or page scroll may conceal the error.
2. **Use only fixture-backed relationship types.** The allowed types named in the supplied record are `supports`, `in tension with`, `exemplifies`, `informs` and `qualifies`. UI pointers from a claim to an action are not new Brain relationships and must not be presented as such.
3. **Do not invent confidence.** If the fixture has no explicit confidence field for a claim, show evidence coverage, freshness and the named gap. Do not derive `high`, `medium`, `low`, a percentage or a reason-labelled confidence class.
4. **Keep `GAP-014` a knowledge-gap annotation.** It may be visibly linked to the relevant route only through an explicit fixture reference. It must not be converted into an uncertain typed edge. If no explicit route link exists, say **Gap not linked to this relationship** and do not choose a relationship from graph shape.
5. **Do not calculate clearance.** Understand, Test and Commit may only change the question asked of existing evidence. They cannot create thresholds, mark an action permitted, force the operator to a lower level or change the recorded next move. Where the fixture is silent, say **No decision rule is recorded**.
6. **Keep the next move recorded, not authorised.** Replace **Use next move** with **Prepare next move** or another local, non-executing action. The proof may reveal or copy fixture-backed preparation material. It may not send, publish, persist or claim that the Brain authorised the move.
7. **Keep private exploration transient.** The private question may filter or highlight existing fixture content only. It must display **Private working view. Does not change the Brain.** No model response, durable node, relationship or interpretation may be implied.
8. **Filter projection before layout.** Customer preview must be built only from objects with established customer visibility. Private text and identifiers must never be rendered and then hidden. If visibility is missing, block preview with the candidate's explicit unavailable state.
9. **Preserve historical non-operability in logic and presentation.** A historical, superseded or rejected interpretation remains inspectable but can never populate the action basis, become selected as current through a casual click or colour the current route as supported.
10. **Bind adverse states to the same frame.** Sparse, quiet, loading, stale, recoverable error and rejected states must keep the decision identity and latest verified meaning visible where available. They must not manufacture urgency, confidence, evidence, a route or a next move.
11. **Make the first read literal.** The opening labels are limited to customer, decision, current meaning, open question and next move. No oversized proclamation, metaphor explanation, self-awarding line or decorative status claim is allowed.
12. **Preserve accessibility without semantic loss.** At 200 percent zoom, switch to the linear claim, evidence, relationship and action order. Keep visible focus, controls at least 44 px, reduced-motion equivalence and text labels for type, direction, history and uncertainty.

## Material founder-gate risk

The winning render can still fail if the Decision Bench becomes a dense generic dashboard. Candidate D has many correct objects, and that is precisely the danger. If the founder sees three equal panels, small labels and analytical furniture before he sees the customer decision, the open question and the preparation move, the reset will have reproduced the same space-allocation failure in a different visual vocabulary.

The cold gate should therefore test one outcome before any explanation: within five seconds, can Krish name the decision, the current supported meaning, the one uncertainty that could change the move and what he will prepare next? If not, the render fails even if every fixture field is present.

Render Candidate D with the binding corrections above. Preserve Candidate H's local relationship route and Candidate P's distinction between belief and decision demand, but keep both subordinate to comparison and preparation.
