## Product synthesis

### Private Brain Builder priority

The founder ranked the cockpit's primary hierarchy:

1. the one thing the customer should be asked or do next;
2. what is useful enough to bring to the customer now;
3. the single best way to deepen the Brain next.

This makes the primary cockpit object a **customer intervention**, not a memory, task, completeness score or system-health metric. The intervention must be traceable to client-ready Brain meaning and the evidence gap it can close. Brain enrichment is therefore purposeful: it exists to improve the next useful customer move, while ready-now value prevents the system from becoming an endless intake programme.

This is a product-hierarchy lock, not approval of an information architecture or rendered surface.

### The near-term user model

For the foreseeable future, the primary operator is Krish and the unit of work is one customer inside the Mindmake thirty-day proof. The customer's Brain is the governed object. The customer is an intentionally lightweight participant who contributes, corrects, reacts and benefits without administering a technical system. Future self-service is enabled by the same kernel but must not distort the near-term operator workflow.

This creates two coupled expressions over one Brain kernel:

1. **Private Brain Builder:** Krish sees current evidence, gaps, uncertainty, staged interpretations, contradictions, enrichment routes, useful interventions and changes since the last evidence.
2. **Customer Brain:** the person sees an unusually insightful, rewarding and explorable account of how they are changing, with lightweight ways to correct, rotate, deepen, act and return.

Neither surface owns separate truth. Operator-only notes and private source material can inform bounded staging, but anything represented as the customer's durable Brain must pass the existing provenance, standing, audience and correction rules.

### State-dependent opening

The opening cannot be one static hero for every customer.

- **Insufficient evidence:** give Krish the one highest-value gap and the easiest credible way to fill it. Still produce a bounded useful observation so the experience does not imply that value is perpetually deferred.
- **Viable Brain:** lead with what changed or became newly useful, then offer the best enrichment route.
- **Rich Brain:** lead with a sharper personal movement, an actionable tension or a growth intervention, with the Living Brain available as the showpiece and inspection layer.

Never display a fake completeness score. The Brain should be “useful now” while being explicit about what one additional signal would make sharper.

### Capture boundary

V1 capture priority is:

1. consented Mindmake meeting transcript;
2. adaptive deeper conversation;
3. one-tap, voice, typed or email-reply customer contribution;
4. Krish's fast voice or note capture.

General work-artifact ingestion and broad work-tool connections are out of scope because they turn the product into enterprise data infrastructure. Email delivery and reply ingestion remain admissible because they reduce participation friction without requiring a work-system integration programme.

### Learning without praise collection

Every meaningful interaction may contribute evidence about usefulness, but interaction is not self-interpreting truth. Open, dwell, save, rotate, correct, act, share and return have different ambiguity and consequence. CTRL may update low-consequence ranking from behavioural evidence, but it must not silently convert a click into a durable claim about the person. Direct correction outweighs passive behaviour. Consequential personal meaning still needs proportionate confirmation.

The customer should be able to rotate an unhelpful insight into a genuinely different interpretation, see that the Brain is still learning, and correct the underlying evidence or meaning when rotation is not enough. No repeated “Was this helpful?” prompts.

## Direct challenge

I disagree with making a one-hour interview the toll gate for usefulness. It is a strong, high-yield accelerator inside the thirty-day proof, but a mandatory extraction ritual creates three risks:

1. it delays the first moment of recognition;
2. it encourages a false sense that one conversation completed the person;
3. it excludes leaders who freeze under explicit intake or prefer asynchronous reflection.

The stronger model is progressive disclosure:

- arrive with a small amount of prior context when ethically and operationally available;
- create a bounded first recognition within the opening conversation;
- use a dynamic guided hour to deepen, contradict and calibrate that recognition;
- continue through tiny, channel-appropriate behaviours during the proof;
- show visible richness upgrades after each meaningful addition.

The one-hour experience becomes valuable work, not the price of admission.
