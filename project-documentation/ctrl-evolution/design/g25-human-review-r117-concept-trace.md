# G25 human review R117 concept trace

Status: selected direction, founder review pending
Date: 2026-09-19
Governing strategy: [G25 human review projection R117](../g25-human-review-projection-r117-strategy.md)

## Decision being designed

R116 proved that the named subject owner can accept, reject and reverse a checked change to their Brain standard. R117 asks a different question: can that exact authority become a leader-facing moment that is clear in seconds, calm on a phone and honest about what will change?

The proof is deliberately narrow. It does not attempt to represent the whole product. It projects one real product responsibility: a person deciding whether a checked learning should become their current standard.

## Shared brief

Every candidate had to show:

- one exact proposed standard change;
- one plain consequence;
- one primary choice owned by the person;
- exact evidence and a countercase on demand;
- honest reversal limits;
- no publication, deployment or operational authority;
- a complete 320px interaction without horizontal overflow; and
- no score, confidence theatre, e-learning language or generic business advice.

## Three materially different spines

### Candidate 1: direct redline

The current and proposed rules appeared together as an immediate before-and-after comparison. It maximised transparency but made the first mobile view dense and forced the leader to parse change syntax before understanding the decision.

### Candidate 2: proposed rule object

The first view contained the proposed rule, its consequence and the choice. Two evidence notches and one `See why` action opened the exact current rule, proposed rule, evidence, countercase and reversal terms. This kept the main decision simple without hiding the record.

### Candidate 3: future-case rehearsal

The leader first saw a realistic future proposal and chose how the rule should apply. The interface then revealed the resulting standard change. This made consequence concrete but introduced a second reasoning task and risked turning the moment into e-learning.

## Blind adjudication

The first judge chose Candidate 1 because the exact change was immediately visible. The second chose Candidate 2 because it was the only direction that remained calm and legible at 320px, provided the complete change could be opened in one action. Both rejected Candidate 3 because it made the leader complete a lesson before reaching the actual authority decision.

A fresh tiebreak selected Candidate 2. The decision was not an average of the two opinions. Candidate 2 preserved the exact delta and evidence in one tap while keeping the first view to one decision. Candidate 1's simultaneous comparison was rejected because it spent the smallest screen on document inspection rather than the consequence and choice.

## Required surgery

The selected direction was allowed to proceed only after these changes:

- keep the synthetic standing visible without repeating it;
- name ownership and reversal in one plain sentence;
- put current wording, proposed wording, consequence, evidence, countercase and reversal in one disclosure;
- use concrete fictional proposal amounts and delays instead of generic quality claims;
- trap focus inside the disclosure, restore focus on close and focus the result after a choice;
- keep every control at least 44px high;
- require review of unusually long wording before approval; and
- verify ready, loading, saving, approved, rejected, stale, error, reversed and long-content states.

## Rejected carry-forwards

R117 must not inherit the struck-through comparison block from Candidate 1, the duplicate impact strip from Candidate 1, or the rehearsal step from Candidate 3. Those devices may be useful elsewhere, but they make this owner-authority moment harder to understand.
