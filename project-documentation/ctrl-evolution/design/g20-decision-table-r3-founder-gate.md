## Root cause

This is a product-rule failure expressed through interaction and copy. R3 asks the leader to decode `rescued`, `quality signal`, `capture step` and `blind comparison` before they can answer. The difficulty comes from the wording rather than the decision.

The Brain should perform that interpretation itself. A leader should only need to recognise a concrete situation, choose a plain answer or add one short example.

## Carry forward

- The approved Decision Table spine and main-screen simplicity.
- One quiet `Ask me more` invitation.
- One route-specific question at a time.
- Three useful input classes: something the leader knows, something the Brain can check and a relevant earlier decision.
- Distinct standing for leader answers, pending evidence and prior-decision warnings inside the Claude brief.
- Optional notes without making free text compulsory.

## Retire

- Abstract free-text questions as the first interaction.
- Internal analytical language in customer-facing prompts.
- Questions that sound intelligent but make the leader work out what is being asked.
- Two-step reveal controls that delay the actual answer.
