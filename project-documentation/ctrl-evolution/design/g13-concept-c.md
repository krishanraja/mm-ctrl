# G13 concept C: The Fold

## Concept in one sentence

The Brain is a pocket atlas that opens at the page most useful to the decision in front of the user: its closed form is a concise current portrait, and one tap unfolds those same statements into a navigable field of evidenced relationships.

## 1. Governing interaction metaphor

**A fold-out personal atlas.**

An atlas has two useful conditions without becoming two products. Closed, it gives a fast editorial reading. Open, it reveals routes between known places. In this concept, each Brain item is a persistent statement tile. The portrait stacks and crops those tiles into a decision-ready reading. The Living Map moves the same tiles into a spatial field and draws only current, evidence-bearing relationships as directly labelled routes.

The fold is functional, not decorative. It is the transition from summary to context. A user never leaves one surface for a separate graph product, and never has to translate between a dashboard card and a different node.

## 2. Exact first 30-second sequence

### 0 to 4 seconds: arrive at the useful page

The opening frame says **“For your marketing decision”** and repeats the active pressure in smaller type: **“How should marketing change for an AI-native category?”**

One statement tile occupies the visual centre:

> Before consequential work ships, the human owns the final judgement and finish.

Above it is the human lens **How I judge**. Beneath it is one dominant action: **“Show why this matters.”** A quiet line at the top says **“Private Brain · current now.”** No count, score, graph or administrative menu competes with the statement.

### 4 to 10 seconds: unfold one meaningful connection

The user taps **“Show why this matters.”** The statement tile holds its position while the page opens sideways around it. The relevant aim tile enters:

> Build a marketing organisation capable of shaping an AI-native category.

A route draws once between the two tiles. Its verb is written on the route: **supports**. Directly below, plain language explains the connection:

> Human release judgement protects the quality of AI-native category work.

The page now offers one dominant action: **“See what this rests on.”**

### 10 to 17 seconds: inspect the basis

The user taps the action. The standard tile turns over in place instead of opening a detached modal. Its reverse shows:

- **You said this**
- the exact supporting statement from the interview
- the interview date and private audience
- **Used for consequential releases**
- **Change this wording**

The source is inspectable before correction and remains the historical basis afterward.

### 17 to 23 seconds: start the correction on the statement

The user taps **“Change this wording.”** The rest of the field quiets. The original sentence remains visible with **“consequential work”** selected. The page asks one question: **“What did you mean?”**

It offers the fixture-grounded correction as the large tap target:

> Externally consequential work needs my final judgement. Reversible internal drafts can stay delegated.

Below it, **“Say or type it differently”** remains available without becoming a chat composer. The dominant action is **“Use this meaning.”**

### 23 to 30 seconds: make the consequence visible before commitment

After the user taps **“Use this meaning,”** the exact durable wording appears. Before anything changes, the point of action states:

> **Your current Brain and its two affected connections will update now.**
>
> **The board brief and shared release will be marked for your review. They will not be rewritten.**

The only dominant action is **“Make this my standard.”** A secondary text action, **“Not yet,”** returns to the unchanged current version.

## 3. Where the user has agency

The system chooses an opening statement because it is relevant to the active decision, but it does not choose truth for the user.

The user controls:

- whether to unfold the portrait into connections;
- which directly related statement to follow next;
- when to turn a statement over and inspect its basis;
- whether to accept the offered narrowing, speak or type different wording, or leave the current version untouched;
- whether to commit after seeing every known downstream consequence; and
- when to review the board brief and shared release that the change flags.

A pending learning appears only as a detached **“For your review”** slip outside the bound atlas page. It has explicit accept, revise and reject actions. It cannot join the portrait or map through silence. A disputed statement remains available in history but cannot appear on a current page or route.

## 4. Primary interaction

**Tap a statement to change its face; tap a labelled route to follow it.**

The three faces of one persistent statement object are:

1. **Read:** its current plain-language meaning in the portrait.
2. **Relate:** its current neighbourhood in the unfolded atlas.
3. **Verify:** applicability, exceptions, evidence and change history on its reverse.

This creates depth without menus or a chat transcript. Nothing requires dragging, hovering, pinching or voice. Keyboard focus follows the same order as the visible route. On desktop, arrow keys may step among connected statements; on mobile, large labelled route targets perform the same movement.

## 5. Information structure

### Closed atlas: current portrait

The portrait is a single ordered reading of at most twelve current, high-value statement tiles. The five human meanings are page markers, not tabs or storage categories:

- What matters
- How I judge
- My calls
- Unresolved
- What changed

The active decision sets the opening page. The user can tap another page marker, but the first frame remains one statement and one action.

### Open atlas: Living Map

The map is not a free-roaming graph canvas. It is a succession of bounded atlas spreads:

- **Orientation spread:** up to eighteen current entities, with the most relevant statement open and the others available as landmarks.
- **Neighbourhood spread:** the selected item plus no more than twenty-three directly relevant or related entities.
- **Statement reverse:** one item, meaning, use conditions, exclusions, exceptions, evidence, history and correction.

Every semantic route carries its relationship verb on the line itself. Tapping the verb reveals its plain-language meaning and evidence. Unrelated items can share a spread but receive no connecting route. Position helps fit and navigate the page only. It does not communicate strength, similarity, certainty or causality. Invisible layout constraints may place tiles, but no decorative tether is drawn.

### Loose material

Proposals and disputed material occupy a separate review leaf behind the current atlas, titled **“For your review.”** Their paper edge, explicit status wording and absence from current routes prevent them from reading as accepted judgement. Superseded versions live behind the statement reverse under **“Earlier wording.”**

## 6. State model

| Surface state | What the user sees | Available action |
|---|---|---|
| Current portrait | One relevant current statement, decision context and page markers | Show why this matters |
| Unfolded orientation | The same statement among the eligible current entities | Choose a statement or return to portrait |
| Relationship focus | Two persistent statement tiles, a labelled route, explanation and evidence entry | Follow either endpoint or inspect the route |
| Statement reverse | Meaning, scope, exceptions, source, history and correction | Change this wording |
| Correction draft | Original meaning and the user's proposed narrower meaning | Use this meaning or revise it |
| Consequence check | Exact new meaning plus affected current views, connections and delivered work | Commit or leave unchanged |
| Rebuilding | The new statement stays anchored; only affected routes pause | No false success action |
| Repaired current state | New wording in place, affected routes redrawn, prior wording in history | Read repair receipt |
| Review required | Board brief and shared release named individually | Review one now or leave for later |
| Proposed | Detached review slip, never bound into the current atlas | Accept, revise or reject |
| Disputed | Removed from current pages and routes; retained in history | Inspect history |
| Private audience rejected | No statement content, routes or cached glimpse | Return or switch to an authorised Brain |
| Loading | Page frame and decision context only, with no invented placeholder claims | Wait or leave |
| Stale | Last verified page with its timestamp and a clear stale marker | Refresh; correction is unavailable until current state is confirmed |
| Recoverable error | The last verified page and any uncommitted wording remain present | Try again; no receipt or change motion appears |

Motion occurs only when state changes: open, focus, correct and repair. Reduced-motion mode replaces folds, turns and route drawing with immediate state changes and short opacity transitions. Focus never moves without the user's action.

## 7. How portrait and Living Map remain visibly one Brain

The identity proof is physical continuity.

- A statement keeps the same typography, silhouette, small human-lens marker and focus ring in both views.
- Unfolding repositions the actual visible tile instead of dissolving it and creating a graph node elsewhere.
- Folding the map closed returns the same tile to the same place in the portrait.
- The portrait and map share one **“current now”** stamp. After correction, both change together and the stamp advances once.
- The map may reveal more eligible items through navigation, but the orientation spread begins with exactly the canonical items represented in the current portrait for the same context.
- The proposed pattern is absent from both accepted views in LB01. It can appear only on the separate review leaf.

For the representative state, the six accepted entities are the marketing decision, aim, current priority, human-release standard, privacy-versus-company-value tension and writing preference. The three visible routes are exactly the evidenced **supports**, **in tension with** and **qualifies** relationships supplied by LB01.

## 8. Correction and repair moment

On **“Make this my standard,”** the old standard does not vanish. It turns face down and slides a few pixels behind the new version at the same atlas coordinate, creating a restrained visible history. The new wording settles on top:

> Before externally consequential work ships, the human owns final judgement and finish; reversible internal drafts may remain delegated.

Only the two affected routes withdraw and redraw:

- **supports:** Human release judgement protects consequential AI-native category work.
- **qualifies:** Distinctive human voice remains part of consequential final craft.

The tension-to-aim route does not move because its meaning did not change. This selective movement makes repair legible rather than theatrical.

A receipt then occupies the lower third of the page:

> **Brain updated**
>
> Portrait rebuilt · Map rebuilt
>
> **Needs your review**
>
> Board brief · Shared release
>
> Nothing already shared was rewritten.

The receipt links each named result to its status. The earlier wording, reason for change and supporting source remain inspectable behind the new statement. Repeating the same correction cannot create another visible version or duplicate receipt.

## 9. Sparse and dense Brains

### Sparse first use

The atlas is allowed to be small. One accepted statement appears as one confident tile with **“No supported connections yet.”** There are no ghost nodes, suggested lines or empty completion mechanics. If the only available material is proposed, the current portrait says **“Nothing is settled here yet”** and offers the separate review slip. Privacy rejection shows no partial content.

### Dense representative scale

At 120 items and 180 relationships, the user never receives all records in one canvas. Orientation remains capped at eighteen entities and a neighbourhood at twenty-four including focus. A tap on a landmark replaces the spread with its bounded neighbourhood. A visible **Back to decision** control restores the original context. Deterministic ordering means the same context produces the same opening page.

On a 390 by 844 pixel screen, long statements wrap inside full-width tiles. The focused item and one selected relationship receive full text; peripheral landmarks use a short first clause plus a clear **Open** target, never an unlabeled dot. On desktop, the same spread gains horizontal room and can expose more of the allowed orientation set without changing selection semantics. The document itself owns scrolling; no card, inspector or map creates a nested scroll region.

## 10. Strongest reason the concept could fail

The fold may feel like memorable choreography but slower than a familiar list once the Brain becomes dense. If users repeatedly want to scan many unrelated judgements, route-by-route navigation could feel restrictive and the physical-continuity idea could consume too much visual space.

The proof should therefore test one hard question: after the first unfold, can a user reach any of the five human meanings and return to the active decision in one tap without losing orientation? If not, the metaphor is imposing itself on the work and should be rejected.

## 11. Constraints that keep it materially distinctive

1. **One object, three faces.** Portrait, relationship view and evidence view must reuse the same persistent statement tile. A summary card beside a separate graph breaks the concept.
2. **No generic graph canvas.** The Living Map is navigated as bounded spreads through labelled routes. Dragging, pinching and arbitrary panning are never required.
3. **Every visible route states its claim.** A line without a relationship verb, plain-language meaning and evidence entry is forbidden. Layout aids remain invisible.
4. **Correction occurs on the statement, not in chat or settings.** The user turns over the exact judgement, changes ordinary-language meaning, sees consequences and commits in place.
5. **Repair is selective and named.** Only affected tiles and routes move. Every rebuilt or review-required dependency appears in the receipt at the correction point.
6. **Unsettled material is literally unbound.** Proposed and disputed material cannot share the visual construction, routes or current stamp of accepted judgement.
7. **No administrative hero.** There is no memory count, progress ring, health score, inbox, persistent composer or dashboard grid on the opening frame.

## Proof feasibility

The concept can be exercised as one self-contained HTML proof using LB01 and LB04. Statement tiles can be ordinary semantic elements; the fold and turn are CSS state changes; relationship routes can be an SVG layer computed from tile positions; focus order can mirror route order; and the correction can swap the supplied fixture state locally and display its four repair outcomes. No production storage, migration or service design is required to test comprehension, interaction, reduced motion, keyboard use and the 390 by 844 review frame.
