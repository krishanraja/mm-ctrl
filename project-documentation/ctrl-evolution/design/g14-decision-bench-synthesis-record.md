## Selection

The Decision Bench governs the next render because it places the consequential job inside the interface: compare current and historical meaning, inspect the evidence, see what remains unknown, then prepare one next move. It uses the wide desktop as a working surface and does not require the operator to learn a new spatial grammar before the Brain becomes useful.

The minority case for the Decision Watershed is retained. It has the strongest visual and semantic expression of the Brain as a living structure. Its best feature travels into the Decision Bench as a local, typed relationship route for the selected claim. The full terrain does not become the governing interaction.

The Decision Cockpit does not travel as a lever or clearance model. Its useful distinction between the best current belief and evidence sufficient for a stronger decision appears as plain language beside the next move. The interface does not compute permission.

## Render structure

At 1440 by 900, the page is one no-scroll private instrument:

1. A compact decision header names Maya, the consequential decision, the current read and the open question.
2. A comparison table shows the current interpretation beside the discarded interpretation. History remains inspectable but cannot guide the move.
3. An evidence bench aligns exact sources to the selected meaning and includes one compact causal change trace.
4. A local Living Brain route exposes only the selected item's fixture-backed one-hop relationships, type, direction and provenance.
5. An action dock keeps the next session move, its basis, the open question and private working controls visible.

No region opens with a large editorial declaration. Labels name objects and actions. The next-move action is `Prepare next move`, not an execution or permission claim.

## Binding fixture guards

- Use only `supports`, `in tension with`, `exemplifies`, `informs` and `qualifies` as Brain relationship types.
- Do not invent confidence percentages or decision thresholds.
- Keep `GAP-014` a separate knowledge-gap annotation.
- Keep private exploration transient and label it as not changing the Brain.
- Build customer projection from customer-visible fields only.
- Historical meaning can be inspected but cannot populate the current action basis.
- At 200 percent zoom, switch to the linear compare, evidence, relationship and action order.
- Sparse, quiet, loading, stale, error and rejected states retain the decision identity and never invent urgency or action.

## Strongest failure risk

The Decision Bench can still fail by becoming a dense generic dashboard. The five-second gate is binding: Krish can identify the decision, current read, open question and next preparation move before decoding controls or small labels. If analytical furniture wins the first glance, the render fails even if every field is present.
