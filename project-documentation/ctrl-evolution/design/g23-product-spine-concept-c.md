# G23 product spine concept C: The Decision Proof

**Revision:** C1
**Date:** 11 September 2026
**Artifact:** Founder alignment concept, not a rendered interface

## Design task contract

- **State of use:** First viewed remotely on a phone or desktop, often between meetings. The founder needs to understand the product in one minute and judge one product spine.
- **User and action:** The founder decides whether this is the right relationship model for a private AI Brain and a thirty-day expert engagement.
- **Governing rule:** The product prepares increasingly useful decisions. The leader sets the purpose, judges the work, makes the call and remains accountable.
- **Data truth:** The starting picture is provisional. Every item shows whether it was said, supported, inferred or is still unknown. Only material with the right standing may shape later work.
- **Materiality:** Material. This concept sets the relationship, information structure and central interaction.
- **Brand and voice:** Plain British English. Visual meaning comes from proof, revision and sign-off. Approved CTRL brand tokens can be applied only after the spine is chosen.
- **Authority:** Approval covers this product spine only. It does not approve final copy, visual execution, implementation, deployment or release.
- **Proof:** Concept specification only. No rendered evidence exists yet. A later material mock would need separate review on phone and desktop.

## Product sentence

A private AI Brain prepares the evidence, questions and choices behind the calls a leader must make about AI and their market, organisation, people and own role. An expert operator tests the thinking. The leader makes the call.

## 1. Governing visual and interaction metaphor

The artifact is a **decision proof**.

A proof is the version placed in front of a responsible person before something consequential becomes final. It can carry sources, questions, corrections and unresolved points. It is useful while still incomplete. It becomes final only through a visible human sign-off.

The whole experience is one working proof sheet with three native areas:

- The **evidence margin** records what the Brain found, where it came from and how much standing it has.
- The **challenge margin** holds the operator's tests, contradictions and judgement about whether an interruption is worth the leader's attention.
- The **decision field** holds the leader's intent, options, consequences, what must remain human and the final call.

The sheet changes through revisions rather than moving through lessons or stages. A later proof visibly carries forward accepted evidence and judgement from earlier work. Proposed interpretations remain in light, provisional form until their standing changes. A correction leaves the old mark visible in the revision record, removes its authority and repairs every later line that depended on it.

This is not styled as publishing software. The proof language is about decisions: intent, evidence, options, consequences, uncertainty and accountable sign-off.

## 2. What the founder sees in the first ten seconds

The opening view has three things and no navigation.

First, the product sentence appears at normal reading size.

Second, one decision proof sits below it. Its three labelled areas make the roles legible at a glance:

| Brain prepares | Operator challenges | Leader decides |
|---|---|---|
| Research, context, patterns, contradictions and uncertainty | Context, pressure tests and the judgement to interrupt | Purpose, success condition, human boundary, taste, final polish and accountability |

Third, a single reveal compares **First proof** with **Later proof**. It does not claim a better outcome. It shows a better-prepared decision:

- First proof: ethical public research, supplied context, explicit gaps and one small hard question.
- Later proof: earlier accepted evidence, remembered judgement, repaired contradictions and relevant outcomes now sharpen the question and comparison.

On desktop the reveal is a horizontal pull across the same sheet. On phone it is a two-state tap. The sheet stays in place so the viewer sees change in the work, not movement through a funnel.

## 3. How the thirty days are understood

The thirty days appear as the revision edge of the proof, like dated marks on a working document. The marks are uneven because real work is uneven.

The first mark is created before the first session from ethical public research and supplied context. Later marks can come from meetings, voice notes, pasted work, documents, email replies, decisions, corrections or outcomes. They are not daily tasks and there is no completion percentage.

Blank space between some marks is intentional. A quiet label reads **No interruption earned**. This makes silence part of the service rather than a missing activity.

Selecting a revision does not open a separate module. It shows what changed on the same proof:

- what arrived;
- what the Brain prepared;
- what question, if any, earned attention;
- what the operator challenged;
- what the leader decided or corrected; and
- what became eligible to help later work.

The first proof is already useful. The relationship deepens through real work, without an extraction phase disguised as onboarding.

## 4. How the three roles remain distinct

The roles are separated by authorship and authority, not by three equal product columns.

**The Brain** moves information and prepares judgement. It researches, organises, compares, notices contradictions, proposes questions, tracks uncertainty and repairs dependent work. Its marks always carry provenance and standing. It cannot sign the decision field.

**The expert operator** works in the challenge margin. They see the rich backstage picture, add context, test weak reasoning and decide whether a question has earned an interruption. They can recommend that an interpretation gains or loses standing, but cannot impersonate the leader's judgement or final voice.

**The leader** owns the decision field. They set the purpose and success condition, decide what remains human, judge validity and consequences, make the consequential call, assess any independent AI review, apply voice and taste, and polish what will ship.

Every mark shows its author. Every consequential action shows its owner. The visual weight increases towards the leader's sign-off, but the preparation remains inspectable.

## 5. How the Brain's compounding becomes visible

Compounding is shown as **reuse with a receipt**.

A small control labelled **Why this appears** reveals the exact earlier material behind any question, option or warning on the current proof. Each receipt states:

- the earlier evidence or judgement;
- who supplied or confirmed it;
- when it gained standing;
- whether it is personal or company material; and
- which current lines depend on it.

The vocabulary remains fixed:

- **Said:** a direct statement or supplied item.
- **Supported:** backed by named evidence.
- **Inferred:** the Brain's proposed interpretation.
- **Unknown:** a material gap.

Standing is separate from type:

- **Provisional:** visible now, barred from shaping later work.
- **Accepted for reuse:** permitted to inform later preparation.
- **Superseded:** preserved in history, no longer permitted to guide current work.

When a leader corrects something, the affected receipts show **Repaired after correction**. The later proof updates in place, while the revision record preserves what changed and why. The visible gain is greater relevance and traceability, not a claim that the Brain has completed a model of the person.

## 6. Where the supporting organs appear

All organs belong to the proof. None becomes a separate destination.

| Organ | Place in the decision proof |
|---|---|
| Low-friction capture | A reply strip attached to the current proof accepts a tap, short text, voice, paste, document or email reply. The Brain drafts first and asks for the smallest useful confirmation. |
| Automatic research and synthesis | The evidence margin assembles public and supplied context before the first session and updates the current proof as relevant material arrives. |
| Adaptive ranked or tappable questioning | One hard question appears inside the decision field only when it could change the call. Ranked choices or taps sit beside a short reason option. |
| Deeper decision comparison | The centre of the proof unfolds into an option comparison using the leader's stated success condition, human boundary, evidence, consequences and uncertainty. It closes back into the same sheet. |
| Prepared intelligence | The proof itself is the prepared intelligence for a decision, meeting or category shift. Each proof states which of the four consequence areas it concerns. |
| Transparent Living Brain | The evidence margin is the inspectable living representation. It can also open as a plain list of accepted, provisional, superseded and unknown material, with provenance and permissions. |
| Evidence, correction and self-repair | Proof marks, standing labels, dependency receipts and the preserved revision record show the mechanism. |
| Portable release or export | The lower edge produces a human-readable proof and a machine-usable package with sources, standing, permissions, corrections and the leader's reasoning. Personal and company material leave as separate owned packages. |
| Quiet periods | Empty revision space carries the explicit label **No interruption earned**. |

Personal and company material never share an implied permission. Their marks use different ownership labels, and a mixed proof can be viewed only within the permissions available to that person. Export separates the two collections rather than flattening them into one Brain file.

## 7. The exact final approval moment

The final moment sits inside the decision field, directly under the comparison and independent AI review. It contains:

- the leader's stated purpose and success condition;
- the chosen call and rejected alternative;
- expected consequences and unresolved uncertainty;
- what remains human;
- the leader's final wording; and
- the label **This is my call**.

Only the leader can select that label. Until then, the proof remains visibly provisional. Selection records the version, reasoning and unresolved points. It does not imply that the Brain or operator made the decision, and it does not turn uncertain material into fact.

For founder review of this concept, the single approval is: **Does the decision proof correctly express the product relationship?** Approval covers the metaphor, role boundaries, revision model, standing model and final sign-off. Everything downstream remains unapproved.

## 8. Mobile treatment

Mobile keeps the same proof but changes its fold.

The first screen contains the product sentence, the three role labels and the First proof or Later proof reveal. The current hard question then occupies one thumb-friendly screen. Evidence and challenge margins open as bottom sheets from clearly labelled controls, with the conclusion placed beside the action that caused it.

The order is stable: Brain preparation, operator challenge, leader decision. Role labels remain visible as each section opens, so stacked content does not blur authorship.

Inputs favour taps, short voice, short text and reply-by-email. No drag is required. Any deeper comparison becomes a vertical sequence of options against the same criteria, with an always-visible return to the call. The page owns the scroll. Components do not. Draft input persists, export remains available and ownership labels never collapse at narrow widths.

## 9. Strongest risk

The proof metaphor could be mistaken for a writing or document-review tool.

The defence is structural, not decorative. The opening sentence names the four AI consequence areas. The centre uses decisions, options, consequences, uncertainty and human boundaries rather than paragraphs under edit. The before-and-later reveal shows judgement becoming better prepared, not copy becoming cleaner.

This risk remains real. If a cold first read produces “document editor” more often than “decision relationship”, the metaphor should be dropped rather than rescued with more explanation.

## 10. Why this is materially different

It is not a case workflow because no single use case owns the spine. Every proof can concern a market, organisation, people or role decision, and the relationship is defined by how evidence and judgement carry across them.

It is not a dashboard because there is no home screen, feed, metric grid, status overview or feature navigation. The user sees one consequential proof at a time and the next earned action sits where the decision happens.

It is not an architecture map because the artifact does not explain components or data movement. It explains authorship, standing, correction, compounding and human accountability through one changing object.

The product is understandable through the relationship: the Brain prepares, the operator challenges, the leader decides, and later work can use only what has earned the right to return.
