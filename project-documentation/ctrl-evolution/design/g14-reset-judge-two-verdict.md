## Decision

After judgement, Markdown line-break whitespace in Candidates T and V was normalised. The hashes above record the normalised artifacts. Their semantic content did not change.

**Winner: Candidate V.** It is the strongest new spine because the Brain's typed, directed relationships organise the decision rather than sitting behind it as a secondary visual. The default route puts the intervention, current meaning, exact support, uncertainty boundary and next inspection in one inspectable field. It also makes uncertainty operational: an unsupported relationship stops the active route instead of merely adding an amber badge.

Candidate T is the strongest alternative. It is immediately useful and makes an important distinction between the best current belief and a safe basis for action, but the three-position commitment model risks implying clearance logic that the fixture does not hold. Candidate X is clear and practical, but its three-pane form is the closest to compressing the rejected direction into a conventional analytical dashboard, and its action gating depends on an uncertainty classification absent from the recorded fixture.

### Candidate T scoring rationale

Candidate T earns the strongest immediate decision-usefulness score. The current answer, largest uncertainty and next permitted move stay together, and the lever lets Krish ask a progressively harder question of the same record. Its main deductions are structural. The Brain remains a window inside the cockpit rather than the thing organising the decision, and `clearance` can feel like machine permission even when the copy says no threshold exists. It is **B+**, becoming **A** if the render proves that the commitment control exposes uncertainty without implying an unrecorded approval system.

### Candidate V scoring rationale

Candidate V is the only concept in which typed, directed relationships simultaneously carry provenance, history, uncertainty and navigation. It uses the wide desktop field for the actual reasoning structure, not for announcement copy, and its gap rule fails honestly when evidence is absent. It loses points because the terrain grammar may be slower to learn than a literal table or lever, and `next inspection` can displace session preparation if the render is not disciplined. It is **A-**, becoming **A** if a cold ten-second test shows that a new viewer can state the decision, current belief, evidence basis and next move without explanation.

### Candidate X scoring rationale

Candidate X is plain and practical. Source-aligned comparison is particularly strong. It loses information-architecture and distinctiveness points because the table, bench, dock and status strip can read as a dense analytical dashboard. More importantly, its invented uncertainty-effect classes weaken evidence honesty and fixture feasibility. It is **C+**, becoming **B+** if action gating is removed unless the fixture explicitly supplies the classification, and if the Brain becomes part of the comparison mechanism rather than a 148 px supporting lens.

## Repetition, regression and discarded strengths

### Candidate T

- **Disguised repetition of the rejected direction?** No. The commitment lever changes the governing action and the whole frame responds in place. This is not the rejected scroll-led intervention sequence with a new skin.
- **Invariant constraint regression?** No outright regression at concept level, but there are two binding risks. A render must not imply clearance thresholds that are absent from the fixture, and it must replace all illustrative relationship names with the five allowed fixture types.
- **Discarded useful strength to restore:** Restore the rejected work's exact causal change trace from latest evidence, to earlier interpretation, to current interpretation, to future test. Put it inside the bearing or evidence readout. Do not recreate it as a long vertical page section.

### Candidate V

- **Disguised repetition of the rejected direction?** No. The relationship route, attention lens and uncertainty stop replace intervention-card sequencing with direct Brain traversal.
- **Invariant constraint regression?** No. It protects history, projection privacy, durable truth, explicit uncertainty and fixture-only relationships more rigorously than the other candidates.
- **Discarded useful strength to restore:** Restore the rejected work's concrete session preparation value, especially the opening, material to bring, listening signal and fallback. Attach a compact fixture-backed preparation line to the selected gap or section cut. Do not add a separate preparation chapter or card stack.

### Candidate X

- **Disguised repetition of the rejected direction?** No, but it is the closest of the three. Cell comparison is a genuinely different primary interaction, yet the persistent current meaning, evidence, action and subordinate Brain regions can become a compressed version of the same hierarchy in dashboard form.
- **Invariant constraint regression?** Yes. The `Blocking`, `Could change this` and `Watch` taxonomy silently adds action-relevant judgment not present in the recorded fixture. That is a data-truth and feasibility regression because it can change whether the next move appears usable.
- **Discarded useful strength to restore:** Restore the rejected work's explicit latest-evidence event and why-now change. Show it as one source-aligned row in the comparison, not as a hero statement or a new causal page section.

## Losing features worth preserving

- **From Candidate T:** Preserve the semantic distinction between `best current belief` and `safe basis for action`. This is stronger than preserving the literal lever. Candidate V can express the distinction at the uncertainty stop without borrowing cockpit styling or inventing clearance.
- **From Candidate X:** Preserve source-aligned comparison of current and historical interpretations. Put that comparison inside Candidate V's section cut for the selected relationship rather than importing the full table, bench and dock layout.

## Render-critical synthesis advice

Render Candidate V as one severe, default route, not as a free-roaming map. At first paint, freeze four readable statements in ordinary language: the intervention, the current interpretation, the exact evidence holding it up and the first uncertainty that limits the next move. Keep one fixture-backed next move beside that gap. The lens should refine this route, not be required to discover it.

Use the right-hand section cut for only three jobs: exact provenance, current versus historical comparison and the compact preparation line. Preserve Candidate T's distinction between current belief and safe action as text at the route stop. Preserve Candidate X's source alignment inside the cut. Do not import the lever, the full comparison table, an action dock or a separate preparation panel. That would recreate the multi-panel collage the earlier synthesis explicitly forbade.

Use only the fixture's allowed relationship types: `supports`, `in tension with`, `exemplifies`, `informs` and `qualifies`. The intervention is a bounded projection object, not a durable Brain item by default, so the render must not fabricate an edge from it into the graph. If the fixture cannot provide a connected route to the intervention, keep the intervention as the fixed decision frame and begin the inspected route at the current canonical item, with the boundary stated plainly.

## Material founder-gate risk

The winning render can still fail if the terrain becomes the new form of desktop theatre. A beautiful map with faint labels, wandering lines and a large empty field would repeat the founder's complaint in spatial rather than editorial form. The failure signal is immediate: after ten cold seconds, Krish cannot state the intervention, why the Brain currently believes it, what remains unresolved and what he should do next.

The call is to render Candidate V once, with the one-route constraint and the fixture boundary above treated as binding. The sharper alternative is Candidate T if the team wants lower comprehension risk and accepts a less Brain-native interaction. The next proof should be a cold 1440 by 900 frame before mobile or decorative motion. The counterpoint is that Candidate T may outperform Candidate V in instant comprehension, but Candidate V has the higher ceiling if the route can stay literal, sparse and action-bearing.
