# G20 reset concept Cobalt: The Decision Table

**Generator:** local isolated concept pass II
**Brief:** `g20-reset-sanitized-brief.md` revision G20-RESET-SB-R1
**Status:** candidate only

## Governing metaphor

A decision table prepared before Maya arrives. Three future operating routes sit on the same surface. The Brain does not summarise them equally. It shows what each route is betting on, where Maya's standards strengthen or weaken it and which unknown could reverse the choice.

## Sequence

### 1. Open on the actual fork

The screen begins with the decision and its synthetic stakes. Maya's current leaning sits above three plain route objects:

- rebuild now;
- prove the new system first; and
- add tools gradually.

Each route has one upside, one failure risk and one evidence weight. No score or percentage appears.

The Brain places a visible marker between the first two routes:

`You are treating speed as the danger. Your evidence says the untested quality system is the bigger risk.`

The marker is linked to Maya's personal pattern: she can diagnose wrongness but has not yet transferred the positive standard.

### 2. Let Maya move the table

Maya taps a route to make it the working route. The other two remain visible. The table immediately answers:

- what this route assumes;
- which of Maya's standards it protects;
- what it puts at risk; and
- what would have to be true for it to win.

`Challenge this route` brings forward the strongest counter-case, not generic pros and cons.

### 3. Turn uncertainty into a test

The Brain recommends one pilot because it resolves four unknowns together: customer value, judgement transfer, role design and incentives. Maya can replace the proposed success evidence or kill condition with voice or one short line.

The table then shows the decision effect plainly: which result would strengthen a larger rebuild, which would weaken it and which would leave the decision open.

### 4. Send the table to Claude

`Design the pilot in Claude` opens a full brief preview. Included context is organised by the same route, assumption, standard, evidence and unknown structure visible on the table. In the proof, the complete brief is copied. No capsule ID or unavailable service is required.

### 5. Bring the plan back to the table

A returned Claude plan lands beside the selected route. The Brain places literal flags on the relevant fields:

- output-volume measure conflicts with the incentive diagnosis;
- no customer proof;
- no budget gate;
- no named human release owner; and
- staged rollout is useful.

Maya can keep the useful fragment, ask Claude to repair the flagged fields or reject the plan. Nothing becomes Brain truth automatically.

## User agency

Maya manipulates the decision, not the memory system. She can choose a route, expose the counter-case and edit what evidence would change her mind. The Brain makes its recommendation visible only after her current leaning is already present.

## Information structure

A spatial comparison owns the wide screen. The selected route expands in place rather than opening another page. The personal pattern, source evidence and future test attach to the route they affect.

On mobile, one route is centred and the adjacent routes remain reachable through labelled previous and next controls. A persistent three-part strip names all routes and the current selection without horizontal dragging.

## Visual language

The table resembles a physical strategy surface rather than a card dashboard. Route lanes share one baseline. Evidence marks physically support or pull against each route. A corrected belief appears as an amber mark crossing out the old causal assumption and reconnecting the evidence to the new one.

Every mark has a visible label. Motion occurs only when Maya changes the selected route or reveals the counter-case. The movement explains how the decision changes.

## State model

`open fork -> working route -> challenged route -> test designed -> external plan -> plan assessed`

Sparse state shows the routes with explicit evidence gaps. Contradiction holds two competing markers rather than averaging them. Stale evidence stays visible but cannot steer the recommendation until refreshed.

## Why it may work

- It immediately operates at the scale of the consequential decision.
- The Brain's personal understanding changes how the strategic routes are interpreted.
- Desktop space carries comparison and causality rather than prose or atmosphere.
- The same structure creates a much stronger Claude brief and return audit.

## Main risks

- It may feel like strategy software rather than a living personal Brain.
- Three routes could imply false completeness if a fourth route has not been considered.
- The table could become analytically dense on a small phone.

## Kill conditions

- The personal pattern feels bolted onto a business decision.
- The routes differ only in pace rather than causal model.
- The surface uses a score to hide weak evidence.
- Mobile requires horizontal drag or loses the comparison.
- The returned plan is criticised generically rather than against a named standard and source.
