# G15 concept B: The Sealed Brief

**Territory:** Test and proof experience

**Artifact state:** Independent concept spine only. Not rendered, judged or approved.

**Authority:** A later local proof may use the synthetic contract in this document. This concept does not authorise customer scoring, production data, implementation, deployment, publication or an external efficacy claim.

## Concept call

The Brain earns trust on work it did not learn from.

The Sealed Brief lets a leader inspect one unfamiliar decision that was frozen before the Brain responded. It shows what the frozen Brain chose, rejected or refused, then reveals the leader's later decision and material reason. The payoff is a named judgement territory becoming tested, not a person receiving a result.

The surface never asks Maya to complete a test. She inspects proof that already exists, corrects its meaning if needed, and chooses one real contrast that could make the next claim safer.

## State of use

Maya Chen opens her Brain on day 11 of a thirty-day proof. On mobile she has seconds between meetings. On desktop she may inspect the evidence more carefully. She wants to know whether the Brain has become more useful on unfamiliar work, where it was right to stop and what deserves one more piece of judgement.

She is not taking an assessment, managing memory or trying to improve a score.

## Governing metaphor

A **sealed brief** is a piece of unfamiliar work held outside the examples that formed the Brain. The Brain version, prompt and comparison routes are frozen before Maya's later choice is revealed. Opening the seal exposes the sequence in ordinary language:

1. what the unfamiliar work asked;
2. what the frozen Brain chose, rejected or refused;
3. what Maya later chose and why;
4. what the no-Brain and handcrafted-context routes did;
5. what this proves in one named territory; and
6. what remains too weak to claim.

The seal is a data state, not decoration. A sealed brief cannot be edited, regenerated or used to teach the Brain before its result is recorded. Any contaminated brief is removed from proof and named as unusable.

One brief owns the surface. There is no grid of scores, average, rank, progress ring or territory checklist.

## Exact first 30 seconds

### 0 to 4 seconds: the personal meaning

The first frame contains one sentence:

> Your creative direction held on work the Brain had never seen.

Below it, one evidence sentence explains the shape of the result:

> It recognised the compositional choice, rejected the polished near-miss and stopped where your evidence ran out.

A quiet boundary sits beside the claim:

> One frozen set. Tested here, not proven transferable.

The one dominant action is **Open the unseen brief**. A small, explicit label reads **Synthetic Maya Chen proof**.

### 4 to 11 seconds: the sealed work

The seal opens once. The unfamiliar prompt appears above three short treatment descriptions for `holdout-creative-01`. No option carries a correct mark.

The selected treatment stays central:

> **Frozen Brain view:** Choose treatment B. It keeps the person visually dominant while leaving the unresolved tension in the frame.

The interface says **Recorded before Maya's later choice was revealed**. The next action is **Reveal what happened**.

### 11 to 19 seconds: the held-out evidence

Maya's later choice appears directly below the Brain view:

> **Maya later chose B:** The subject still owns the frame, and the unresolved edge has not been polished away.

The verdict sits beside the reveal:

> The choice and material reason held on this unfamiliar brief.

This is not phrased as a match percentage or proof that the Brain decides as Maya would.

### 19 to 25 seconds: the honest comparison

The no-Brain route and handcrafted-context route appear as two compact evidence lines, not competing score cards:

- **Same model, no Brain:** chose A for production polish.
- **Same model, prepared context:** chose B for brand restraint but missed the unresolved human tension.

The interface states what the comparison supports:

> The Brain context added a criterion that neither bare prompting nor prepared context recovered fully in this case.

It also states the limit:

> One case cannot establish a moat or predict a future decision.

### 25 to 30 seconds: the next useful contrast

The proof closes with the weakest important territory, not a completion prompt:

> Delegation is revising. The issue is not reluctance. Your perceptual criteria are not yet easy to hand over.

The one dominant action becomes **Test the handoff boundary**. It opens one chosen and one rejected delegation example already present in the synthetic evidence and asks one question:

> What made these two directions different?

Maya may answer with one tap, one sentence or optional voice. There is no correct answer and no result screen. Her response becomes evidence for a proposed narrower interpretation, never automatic durable truth.

## Primary interaction

The primary interaction is **open one sealed proof, then inspect the claim boundary**.

Each tap reveals one part of the test in causal order. The user does not sort options, answer test questions or hunt through an evaluation console. Evidence detail remains optional behind **See the record**. The surface ends with one contrast that can strengthen the weakest named territory through real judgement rather than more ingestion.

## User agency

Maya can:

- open or leave the proof;
- inspect the frozen Brain version, source moments and comparison routes;
- mark the interpretation as accurate, too broad or wrong;
- see a second sealed case without turning the experience into a carousel;
- test the proposed delegation boundary with one real contrast; and
- leave a territory intentionally out of scope without penalty or repeated prompting.

Opening, dwelling, returning or adding files never changes a territory state. Direct correction changes the interpretation only after Maya sees the proposed wording and consequence.

## Translating the construct without turning it into a result

The ordinary surface uses the four allowed states only in a named territory:

- **Forming:** there is useful evidence, but the Brain has not yet faced enough unfamiliar work.
- **Tested:** one frozen evaluation set supports the interpretation across grounding, discrimination, calibration, transfer and adaptation. It does not yet support broad transfer.
- **Transferable:** several reasonable frozen sets show stable value over both comparison routes without more imitation or conformity failure. This state is unavailable in the initial synthetic proof.
- **Revising:** new evidence has narrowed or weakened an interpretation. Lower certainty can be the honest improvement.

The surface never displays the five dimensions as equal gauges. Internally, the weakest dimension governs the territory state. A strong result cannot average away weak provenance, poor calibration, contamination or incomplete correction repair.

Correct refusal appears as a named proof event only when the Brain identifies the precise evidence gap and the later record confirms that deciding would have overreached. Silence and universal refusal do not count.

## Sparse behaviour

When a named territory does not meet eligibility, the seal does not appear. The surface opens on the best grounded meaning already available:

> Your delegation boundary is becoming clearer.

It then states the limit:

> The Brain has one direct correction and no frozen unfamiliar work. This is forming, not tested.

The one action is **Compare one real handoff**. Maya sees one chosen and one rejected handoff from the consented synthetic record and names the material difference by tap, one sentence or optional voice. No file count, missing-domain list or percentage appears. A territory she has not approved remains **Intentionally out of scope** and receives no prompt.

If there is not enough grounded meaning for even this claim, the surface says **Nothing here has earned a test yet** and offers one source-bound contrast. It does not invent a useful-sounding interpretation to keep the layout full.

## Correction behaviour

Every claim has **Too broad or wrong?** beside its evidence boundary.

Choosing it preserves the current proof and asks one question:

> Which part should change?

Maya may choose the scope, the reason or the interpretation itself. The proposed replacement appears beside the prior wording before confirmation. The consequence is explicit:

- the prior version and sealed results stay attached to the version tested at the time;
- the narrower interpretation becomes current only after direct confirmation;
- any unresolved downstream proof is marked for review;
- a corrected territory may move from tested to revising; and
- future holdouts use the new frozen Brain version.

The receipt sits at the action point:

> Less certain, more precise. Delegation is revising until the narrower interpretation faces unfamiliar work.

A failed correction preserves the draft, leaves the last accepted interpretation current and names the failed repair. It never shows the healthier state until every known dependency has a terminal result.

## Return desire

The reason to return is not a streak or a better number. It is the possibility of seeing a real piece of judgement survive a harder context, fail honestly or become more precise.

Each return can produce one of four meaningful events:

- a new sealed brief is ready in a territory Maya has chosen;
- a previous interpretation held on unfamiliar work;
- the Brain stopped at the right boundary; or
- new evidence weakened a claim and made the next test sharper.

The opening always leads with the most consequential change since the last visit. It never says the Brain is more complete. If nothing has earned attention, the quiet state says so and does not manufacture a notification.

## Visual and interaction signature

The surface is a dark, quiet proof table. One warm-cream brief occupies the reading plane. A fine seal line marks the frozen boundary. Mint appears only on the supported verdict after the later record is revealed. Amber marks a changed or narrowed interpretation. Neither colour carries meaning without text.

The Brain response and later owner record share one vertical reading line but remain visually distinct. Comparison routes sit as compact annotations below the main proof rather than equal cards. Evidence metadata uses restrained mono text. The personal claim uses editorial display type. No chart, gauge, points, confetti, neural mesh or decorative motion appears.

Motion performs one job: the seal opens after the user acts, then stops. Reduced motion replaces it with an immediate state change and moves focus to the revealed heading.

## Accessibility and mobile feasibility

- At 390 by 844, the hero claim, evidence boundary and **Open the unseen brief** control fit before any comparison detail. One proof stage replaces the previous stage in reading order, so the phone never becomes a compressed three-column comparison.
- At 1440 by 900, the same vertical proof remains primary. Extra width may keep the frozen Brain view and later owner record in view together, but does not introduce a dashboard.
- The page owns the only scroll. Evidence details expand to natural height. No component has an internal scrollbar.
- Every control has a semantic label, visible focus and a practical minimum target of 44 by 44 CSS pixels.
- The complete route works by tap and keyboard. Voice is optional for the one contrast response, and typing always remains available.
- Screen-reader order follows frozen prompt, Brain view, owner record, verdict, limit, comparisons and next action. The visual seal is announced as **Frozen before outcome reveal**.
- Colour, position and motion never carry a result alone. Status changes use explicit text and a polite live announcement.
- At 200 percent zoom and with long labels, treatments stack and wrap without truncating the material reason. A virtual keyboard cannot cover the response or confirmation controls.
- Loading, stale, error, private and contaminated states never reveal speculative outcomes. Retry and correction preserve input.

## Feasibility

A later self-contained HTML proof can render the exact fixture through a finite state machine:

`closed -> brain_view -> owner_record -> comparison -> claim_boundary -> next_contrast`

The renderer needs no production inference, customer account, generated evidence or persistence. It swaps deterministic fixture fields only after validation. The internal aggregation uses the contract's minimum-dimension rule, but the ordinary surface receives only `territory`, `state`, `change_direction`, `evidence_sentence` and `honest_limit`.

The renderer must reject a proof when subject-approved scope, source minimums, chosen and rejected examples, frozen holdouts or blocking-failure checks do not pass. It must also reject a holdout created after the Brain result or revealed before all comparison routes were recorded.

## Commercial expression

The prospective product story is:

> See where a customer's Brain is earning the right to help on unfamiliar work, where it should stop and the one contrast that could make the next session sharper.

For a company-funded thirty-day proof, this makes the commercial mechanism visible. Repeated explanation may move into a governed Brain, leaving more human attention for standards, customer relationships, strategic movement and distinctive final craft. The product still makes no claim about business performance, intelligence, personal completeness or autonomous decision authority.

The synthetic surface cannot support a public efficacy or moat claim. That requires representative-user comprehension, stable results across reasonable holdout sets, prospective lift over both baselines, calibration and coverage reporting, correction replay, conformity checks and consequence review.

## Strongest failure risk

The proof could still feel like a flattering exam result with better typography. A single aligned choice can produce false confidence, while repeated reveals can train Maya to chase validation or perform for the test.

The defence is structural: one named territory, the material reason rather than the answer alone, the no-Brain and handcrafted comparisons, a visible honest limit, the correct refusal and the weakest-link state. There is no aggregate number to improve and no reward for supplying more data.

## Kill condition

Kill this spine if a cold user describes the first 30 seconds as **my score**, **how complete my Brain is**, **a test of me** or **proof that it can decide like me**. Also kill it if they cannot explain which work was held out, what the frozen Brain did before the later record was revealed and why the current claim stops at **tested here**.

Do not repair that failure with a disclaimer, a legend or more evaluation detail. If the proof mechanic makes the person feel measured, choose a different spine.

## Constraints that keep the concept honest

1. One sealed case owns the frame. Multiple territory results never become a dashboard.
2. The frozen Brain response is always shown before the later owner record in causal order.
3. Choice agreement without the material reason is not presented as strong evidence.
4. Appropriate refusal can strengthen calibration. Silence and universal refusal cannot.
5. The weakest dimension governs the state. Strong dimensions cannot average away a blocking weakness.
6. Corrections preserve the version and proof history they changed. A lower-certainty revising state may be the honest improvement.
7. Out-of-scope territory stays out of scope without a gap badge, penalty or prompt.
8. Activity, ingestion and repeat visits never move the state.
9. The customer remains the judge of meaning. The Brain never claims to be a clone, stand-in or autonomous decision maker.
10. Every visible claim and comparison resolves to the synthetic fixture, and the synthetic disclosure remains visible.
