# G14 Concept A: The Intervention Lens

**Generator:** root design producer, isolated concept pass A

**Status:** unselected concept spine

## Governing metaphor

A precision instrument that brings one customer move into focus. The rest of the Brain stays present as a dim evidence field, but the operator sees only the intervention currently sharp enough to deserve the next session.

## Sequence

1. Open directly on one intervention expressed as a decisive sentence.
2. Reveal why this move is timely through a three-part causal trace: new evidence, changed interpretation, expected learning.
3. Let Krish prepare the exact contrast, question or exercise.
4. Offer one quiet route to inspect evidence or ask the Brain privately.
5. After use, ingest the session evidence and replace the lens with a change receipt.

## User agency

The Brain selects one best move. Krish can use it, reshape it, rotate to a genuinely different route, or reject the underlying interpretation. Rotation is a consequential branch, not a carousel of similar suggestions.

## Primary interaction

Focus, prepare, then commit. A single action expands the intervention into a session-ready object with exact language, material to bring, the signal to listen for and what the result would teach the Brain.

## Information structure

- Hero: one supported intervention.
- Causal rail: evidence -> changed meaning -> intervention -> learning.
- Ready-now shelf: one customer-facing insight or contrast worth bringing.
- Deepen route: one missing signal and its lowest-friction capture path.
- Private ask drawer: context-bound question to the Brain.
- Customer preview: what the intervention could return to the customer.

## State model

Event-driven. `evidence_arrived -> interpretation_revised -> intervention_ready -> prepared -> awaiting_session -> evidence_returned`.

Sparse state still gives one bounded observation and one evidence request. Quiet state explicitly says no interruption has earned attention. Stale state blocks confident preparation until the source is refreshed.

## Visual signature

One large typographic intervention sits inside a restrained circular focus field. Fine evidence lines converge toward it without implying semantic relationships. Amber marks what moved; mint marks what is supported and ready. The causal rail behaves like an optical calibration strip rather than a progress tracker.

## Strength

Fastest ten-second comprehension and strongest protection against dashboard behaviour. It turns the Brain into a useful intervention engine rather than a memory browser.

## Risk

It may over-concentrate authority in the model-selected move and underplay the customer's living personal portrait. If the central recommendation is weak, the whole product feels weak.

## Feasibility

High. The renderer needs a bounded intervention object, evidence refs, one change event, one deepening route and state-specific fallbacks. It can sit over the existing Brain kernel without new graph semantics.
