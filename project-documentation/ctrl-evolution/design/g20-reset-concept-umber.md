# G20 reset concept Umber: The Second Pass

**Generator:** local isolated concept pass III
**Brief:** `g20-reset-sanitized-brief.md` revision G20-RESET-SB-R1
**Status:** candidate only

## Governing metaphor

The Brain proves itself by improving real work. Maya opens a proposed operating plan and sees a second pass laid over it in her own standards: what is generic, what is unsupported, what conflicts with her judgement and what is genuinely new. The personal Brain becomes visible through the quality of its discrimination.

## Sequence

### 1. Begin with the work

The opening shows the deliberately generic Claude answer as a short plan. Four phrases carry quiet underlines. A margin says only:

`Maya's Brain would stop this here.`

The first annotation opens automatically:

`Track engagement and output repeats the incentive problem you are trying to remove.`

The source is the current synthetic scorecard. Maya can move through each annotation with one tap.

### 2. Reveal the standard behind the judgement

Each annotation names one personal standard in plain language:

- only Aperture House would say it;
- specific before confident;
- do not turn taste into a formula; and
- name the human who owns the final call.

Maya sees whether the standard came from her direct words, observed work, a correction or a provisional Brain read. `Not my standard` creates a correction at the exact annotation.

### 3. Show what the Brain has learned about Maya

After the fourth annotation, the plan folds into a concise personal portrait:

`You are not trying to automate marketing. You are trying to make your way of seeing quality travel before the work reaches you.`

Three linked patterns show how the conclusion was formed and one counter-case warns that incentives may still matter more than judgement transfer.

### 4. Rebuild the request

The Brain converts the failed plan into a better Claude request. The user sees the exact additions as five coloured insertions: customer proof, competing operating models, human judgement points, budget gate and kill condition.

`Try again in Claude` copies the complete self-contained brief. The preview can also show the difference between the weak request and the rebuilt one without requiring Maya to read the complete payload.

### 5. Compare the return

When a new answer returns, the same second-pass layer checks it. The proof includes a repaired result with one remaining unknown. Maya chooses whether the work is closer, whether the Brain missed something and which judgement still belongs to her.

The closing state records one learning only if Maya corrects or confirms a standard. A clean AI answer does not increase Brain completeness by itself.

## User agency

Maya judges both the external work and the Brain's critique. She can reject any claimed standard at the point it is applied. The system learns from her discrimination, not from passive agreement with generated prose.

## Information structure

The returned artifact is the primary canvas. Brain meaning appears as annotations attached to exact words. The personal portrait is earned after the product demonstrates its judgement rather than announced first.

Desktop uses a wide work surface with a narrow annotation margin. Mobile shows one highlighted passage and its annotation at a time, with the rest of the plan still readable in natural page flow.

## Visual language

Warm paper holds the external work. Mint underlines mean supported alignment. Amber underlines mean an unresolved or conflicting choice. A fine red-brown strike means the phrase fails an accepted standard. No abstract image appears. The only visual spectacle is meaning being attached to the exact work.

## State model

`unassessed work -> annotated work -> disputed or accepted standard -> rebuilt request -> reassessed work -> optional learning`

Sparse state shows only the standards the Brain can support. If no standard applies, the system says it cannot judge the work yet and asks for one concrete comparison. Wrong-customer and stale-standard states disable the overlay rather than guessing.

## Why it may work

- It demonstrates augmentation before explaining the Brain.
- It makes taste, standards and judgement concrete and inspectable.
- It directly solves the generic-output failure that triggered the reset.
- It can teach Maya about her own judgement without becoming a course or self-assessment.

## Main risks

- Beginning with bad AI work may make the product feel reactive rather than visionary.
- The larger strategic decision can disappear behind line editing if the selected artifact is too tactical.
- Annotation density can become fussy or editorial on mobile.

## Kill conditions

- The critique focuses on prose instead of the decision's causal model.
- The Brain portrait is merely a flattering summary of the annotations.
- The system claims a standard without showing how Maya established or corrected it.
- The rebuilt Claude brief is hidden or still depends on an external resolver.
- The repaired answer appears final without a visible human judgement point.
