## Concept B: The Sealed Brief

### Judgement

This is the strongest proof mechanism and the easiest commercial demonstration. The causal order is excellent: unseen work, frozen Brain response, Maya's later choice, comparison routes, claim boundary, then one next contrast. A user can understand what was tested and why the claim stops at "tested here" without seeing a score.

It loses on felt product value because the dominant interaction is still an outcome reveal. Repeated use could become a tasteful validation loop in which Maya waits to see whether the Brain got her right. That is close to an exam dynamic even though she does not answer the test herself.

There is also a concrete provenance defect in the supplied contract. `brain-maya-v1` is recorded on 29 August but cites `source-session-d02`, captured on 30 August, and `source-chosen-d04`, captured on 1 September. A frozen version cannot be grounded in later evidence. Because the concept's trust claim depends on temporal integrity, this is a material metric-truth deduction. The renderer's stated fail-closed behaviour should reject that version until the lineage is repaired.

## Concept C: The Becoming Receipt

### Judgement

This best satisfies the brief's first-frame requirement. "Delegation looks different now" is personally legible, the before-and-now sequence makes the correction understandable, and the honest limit is present before any evidence appendix. The receipt only exists after a material change, so ingestion and repeat visits cannot masquerade as progress.

The append-only record is unusually well matched to correction. It preserves the former interpretation, names the narrower current reading, keeps unrun holdouts from awarding progress, and places one low-cost contrast at the end. The linear document model is also the most credible at 390 by 844, 200 percent zoom and screen-reader order.

Its main weakness is that a receipt book can decay into a tasteful activity timeline. Dates and accumulating entries may become the implicit progress meter even without a total. The product must keep the issuance threshold strict and ensure every receipt changes a present interpretation, claim boundary or next action.

## Concept A: The Focus Plane

### Judgement

This is the most distinctive concept and makes the correction emotionally immediate. The fixed central meaning with Chosen, Rejected, Unfamiliar and Corrected around it is a coherent information model, not a score chart. "Less certainty, better fit" is especially strong.

The cost is comprehension and implementation fragility. On mobile the spatial grammar becomes a two-by-two control set, and at 200 percent zoom it becomes a linear document. That means the concept's defining advantage weakens precisely in the primary and accessibility-critical states. Terms such as focus, sharper and forming also carry an avoidable competence metaphor. Users may read visual focus as personal quality even when every label is careful.

Its data contract also places an `operator_private` held-out evaluation receipt in the evidence used by the customer-facing unfamiliar-work plane. A derived outcome may be shareable, but the contract does not establish that authority. The proof must fail closed until the audience boundary for that result is explicit.

### Hard-failure test

- Personal completeness or deficiency: pass in the written specification, but this is the highest cold-comprehension risk.
- Activity, volume or agreement rewarded: pass.
- Blocking weakness hidden in an average: pass.
- Number made the motivational hero: pass.
- Healthy uncertainty treated as failure: pass.
- Invented or unavailable data required: pass, subject to resolving the audience authority of the held-out receipt.
- Dashboard, quiz result or generic AI visualisation: pass in specification, but the rendered proof must show that the compass is understood as evidence-role navigation rather than a generic AI map.

## Strongest reason the winner could fail

The Becoming Receipt could become retrospective content rather than a living product mechanism. If Maya reads receipts as an elegant changelog, scans past entries, and feels no consequence for the next real decision, return desire will collapse into passive curiosity. The decisive cold test is whether users can say not only what changed, but what they would now do differently or test next.

## Bounded borrowing decision

**Borrow one mechanism from Concept B:** inside Concept C's existing exact-evidence appendix, show one held-out case in strict causal order: frozen Brain response first, Maya's later record second, then the claim boundary. Borrow only this ordering rule, not the seal metaphor, staged reveal theatre, comparison-led first frame or proof-table visual language.

This materially improves trust in the receipt without creating a feature collage because it occupies an appendix Concept C already requires. The receipt remains the governing spine, the correction remains the first-frame meaning, and the borrowed mechanism answers one narrow question: was this claim earned on work the Brain could not have learned from?

Do not borrow from Concept A. Its evidence-role compass would duplicate the receipt's chronology and appendix while weakening the winner's linear mobile clarity.

## Final recommendation

Advance Concept C to the playable synthesis, with three non-negotiable gates:

1. A receipt is issued only for a material interpretation, evaluation, correction or calibrated refusal, never for ingestion or elapsed time.
2. The first frame keeps the current change and honest limit above history, dates and technical proof.
3. The exact-evidence appendix uses Concept B's frozen-before-owner causal ordering and fails closed on provenance, audience or repair ambiguity.
