# G20 concept C: The Context Aperture

**Artifact revision:** G20-C-APERTURE-R1  
**Rendered evidence:** None. Concept document only  
**Approval state:** Proposed and unrendered  
**Downstream owner:** G20 synthesis and, if selected, one synthetic rendered proof  
**Pending action:** Judge the interaction spine against the G20 sanitized brief before any render or implementation

## Design task contract

**STATE OF USE:** Krish is inside one customer's private Decision Bench, usually on desktop between sessions and sometimes on mobile. He has a fragment to preserve or needs a small, relevant part of the Brain in Claude now. He is busy, may be holding the phone in one hand and should not have to administer an integration.

**USER + ACTION:** Krish captures one fragment in one deliberate action, understands the private staging receipt, narrows Maya's Brain to the minimum context for a named task, uses that context in Claude and brings one result back as attributed staged material.

**GOVERNING RULE:** The active customer, purpose and audience stay visible while one aperture determines what crosses the boundary. Anything outside the opening does not leave CTRL. Anything returning through it lands in private staging and cannot become trusted Brain truth.

**DATA TRUTH:** The proof uses synthetic Maya Chen data only. It may show the consequential decision, current supported read, open question, fixture-backed source references, one explicit exclusion, audience, freshness, expiry and four-way return provenance. It may not imply that the context is complete, that Claude was automatically prompted, that a returned suggestion is correct or that customer value has been proved.

**MATERIALITY:** Material interaction concept.

**BRAND + VOICE:** Preserve the approved dark, warm, instrument-like Mindmake environment and icon-only shell. Mint means the current answer or a completed safe handoff. Amber means newly captured, changed or returned staged material. Visible copy is plain and human. Technical identifiers remain in deliberate detail inspection only.

**AUTHORITY:** Concept document only. No render, implementation, connector setup, account action, production schema, Brain write, customer data, deployment or release is authorised.

**PROOF IF SELECTED:** A synthetic self-contained interaction at 1440 by 900, 390 by 844 and 320 by 568. It must cover every required state, complete keyboard and touch paths, visible focus, controls of at least 44 px, reduced-motion equivalence, no nested scrollbars, no hidden active controls and scripted closure of wrong-customer and expired-context failures.

## 1. Governing interaction metaphor

The Context Aperture is one purpose-shaped opening through the Brain.

The Decision Bench remains visible as the working environment. When Krish chooses to move context, a bounded aperture opens over the focused Living Brain route. Brain material remains faintly visible around it, but only the items inside the opening are named under **Claude will see**. A clear outer band names **Stays in CTRL**. The distinction is spatial, written and inspectable. It does not depend on colour.

The aperture has two directions:

- **Inward:** new material crosses into CTRL and lands in private staging.
- **Outward:** a small, purpose-bound Brain slice becomes available to Claude through the read-only path.

The same opening receives a result on return. Direction changes, but the trust rule does not. Crossing the aperture never creates trusted memory.

The aperture is not a permissions editor. Krish does not manage scopes, tokens, connectors or access lists. He names or accepts the work purpose, sees the proposed minimum context, removes or adds an explicitly relevant item and performs one clear action. Technical detail is available only under **Inspect details**.

## 2. First five seconds

At rest, the Decision Bench shows its normal decision work. A compact exchange mark sits beside the current decision, not in global navigation. Opening it preserves four fixed facts at the top of the aperture:

1. **Maya Chen, Aperture House**
2. **Private operator view**
3. **Decision: make Maya's quality standard usable by the team**
4. **Nothing moves until you choose**

The aperture opens in its last deliberate direction, but never with a previous customer's contents. On first use it opens inward with one literal prompt:

**Add something to Maya's Brain**

Paste is ready immediately. File and voice are visible as equivalent input paths, not as a connector toolbar. A quiet secondary route says **Use Brain context in Claude**.

Nothing is copied, shared or submitted at first paint. No whole-Brain option is present.

## 3. Primary interaction

The primary interaction is **setting the aperture by purpose, then acting on exactly what remains inside it**.

For capture, the input itself sets the aperture. A paste, drop, chosen file or completed voice recording crosses inward and immediately becomes an amber staging receipt. The system asks nothing unless ambiguity changes customer, audience, provenance or meaning.

For Claude, the current Decision Bench focus proposes the purpose:

**Help make Maya's quality standard usable by her team**

Accepting that purpose contracts the visible Brain to a deterministic, fixture-backed slice. The selected current read, open question and at least three linked source references sit inside the opening. At least one operator-private item remains visibly outside with its reason. Krish can inspect the cut, remove an included item or add one directly connected fixture item. There is no select-all action.

The verdict sits beside the action:

**Claude will receive 5 items for this task. Krish's private session note stays here.**

The one primary action is then **Use this context** when the read-only connector path is available, or **Copy task starter** when it is not. The copied starter contains the task, active customer label and opaque context reference only. It contains no private Brain content.

## 4. Sequencing and state transitions

The surface is a small state machine. Each state has one question or one action.

### A. Ready

The active customer and private boundary are fixed. The inward aperture is ready for paste. File and voice are alternatives for the same ask.

Transition: one paste, drop, file choice or completed voice recording moves to **Received**.

### B. Received

An immediate amber receipt shows:

- what arrived;
- source form and capture time;
- **Private staging**;
- what CTRL recognised from the fixture-backed proof;
- what CTRL did not assume;
- whether processing is complete, delayed or unnecessary.

The primary action is **Done**. If one material ambiguity exists, the state moves to **One question** instead.

### C. One question

The aperture stops around the exact ambiguous element. It asks only the route-changing question, for example:

**Is this about Maya's team quality standard?**

The answers are short chips: **Yes**, **Different Maya topic** and **Wrong customer**. The original input remains intact while the answer is pending.

Transition: one answer returns to the receipt or closes safely.

### D. Set purpose

The aperture faces outward. It proposes the current Decision Bench purpose in plain language. Krish may accept it or replace it with one sentence. There is no prompt-engineering form.

Transition: **Use this purpose** moves to **Review cut**.

### E. Review cut

The Brain remains visible around the opening. Inside it are only the fixture-backed items proposed for the purpose. Outside it are excluded material and the rest of the Brain. The current cut shows:

- current supported read: **The team misses early quality signals**;
- open question: **What is the first warning sign Maya notices?**;
- at least three selected source references;
- one explicit operator-private exclusion;
- audience: **Claude for Krish's private work**;
- freshness from the fixture;
- exact expiry time;
- the named purpose.

Removing an item tightens the aperture immediately. Adding is limited to directly connected fixture items shown at the edge. The primary action remains beside the updated verdict.

Transition: **Use this context** moves to **Ready in Claude** when the read-only path is available. If it is not, the state becomes **Copy fallback**.

### F. Ready in Claude

A mint completion line states what happened without claiming that Claude has acted:

**Context is ready for Claude. Nothing was submitted.**

The screen shows the capsule audience, freshness and expiry in plain language. **Copy task starter** remains available. Revocation is one direct action from this receipt, not a settings journey.

Transition: a returned result pasted or deliberately received through the same aperture moves to **Returned**.

### G. Copy fallback

The aperture stays on the reviewed cut. The only active action is **Copy task starter**. After copying, the receipt states:

**Task starter copied. It contains no Brain contents.**

The next instruction is literal: paste it into Claude, where the read-only connector can retrieve the named context if available. If the connector is unavailable there too, the user may deliberately choose **Export this cut**, which is a separate manual action and lists the exact material that will enter the clipboard before it happens.

### H. Returned

The result enters through the aperture with an amber edge and lands in **Private staging**. The receipt says:

**Claude suggestion received. It is a source, not Brain truth.**

The source chain distinguishes:

- Maya's words;
- Krish's judgement;
- CTRL synthesis;
- Claude suggestion.

No accept-to-memory control is present in this first surface. The primary action is **Keep in staging**. Later governed Brain review remains outside this concept.

## 5. Desktop information structure

At 1440 by 900, the approved Decision Bench remains no-scroll. The aperture opens over its focused evidence and Living Brain region while leaving the customer identity, current decision and next-move context visible.

### Fixed identity strip

The existing icon-only shell remains unchanged. A narrow strip above the aperture shows the active customer, company, private audience and current decision. It never collapses while context is moving.

### Brain field

The left two-thirds of the exchange surface shows the current fixture-backed Living Brain route. The current interpretation, open question, sources and operator-private note retain their existing typed and directed relationships. The aperture is drawn as restrained linework across that field. Items inside remain at reading contrast. Items outside recede but remain named when focused.

### Exchange receipt

The right third is a natural-height aligned receipt. It does not scroll inside itself. Its content changes with the current state:

- inward receipt: captured item, private staging, understood, not assumed;
- outward receipt: purpose, Claude will see, stays in CTRL, freshness, expiry;
- return receipt: Claude attribution, private staging, no trusted-memory effect.

The current verdict and primary action share the same block. The user never acts in one region and searches elsewhere for the consequence.

### Aperture control

The aperture is adjusted by selecting named Brain objects at its edge, not by manipulating an abstract privacy slider. Pointer users click or tap **Include** and **Keep here** on the focused object. Keyboard users move through the proposed cut in semantic order and use the same named actions. A short line movement shows the opening change. Reduced motion updates it instantly.

There is no nested scroll. Deliberate detail inspection may replace the receipt temporarily, with **Back to context cut** returning to the same state.

## 6. Mobile behaviour

Mobile turns the aperture into a vertical cut without changing its meaning.

The sticky top line preserves **Maya Chen**, **Private** and the current decision. The first screen contains one ask only.

- In capture mode, the input target and paste, file and voice alternatives appear first. The receipt replaces the input after capture.
- In Claude mode, the purpose appears first, followed by **Claude will see**. The aperture becomes a written boundary between **Included** and **Stays in CTRL**.
- In return mode, the source chain and **Private staging** appear before any detail.

The full Brain map is not squeezed onto mobile. Included items appear as an ordered relationship route with written type and direction. Each item has a 44 px or larger **Include** or **Keep here** control. Content uses natural page movement with no nested scroller. At 320 by 568, the active customer, audience, one ask and its consequence remain visible before supporting detail.

Voice is a first-class path. If the proof can record but not transcribe, the receipt must say **Audio saved. Words not transcribed**. If recording is unavailable, the voice control is visibly unavailable with **Voice is not available in this browser** and cannot look active.

## 7. How the boundaries remain legible

### What was captured

The inward receipt echoes the original fragment or file identity, source form, capture time and private staging destination. It states the narrow interpretation CTRL can support and a separate **Not assumed** line. The original remains available through **View original** during every recoverable state.

### What Claude receives

The outward aperture is the answer. Every included item is both visibly inside the opening and listed under **Claude will see**. Every explicit exclusion is visibly outside and listed under **Stays in CTRL**. Audience, freshness, expiry and purpose remain next to the primary action. Technical capsule or provenance identifiers are hidden under **Inspect details**.

### What came back

The return receipt names Claude as the authoring source and shows the chain of prior contributions without flattening them. Amber marks the result as new staged material. Mint is withheld because the result is neither verified nor accepted. The absence of any direct Brain-write action makes the state model match the trust claim.

## 8. Required states

| Required state | Aperture behaviour | One primary action |
|---|---|---|
| Ready to capture | Inward opening, Maya and Private fixed, paste focused, file and voice visible as alternate input paths | Paste, drop, choose file or finish recording |
| Pasted material received | Amber receipt echoes the original, private staging, understood and not assumed | **Done** |
| Ambiguity needs one answer | Opening stops around the ambiguous subject, audience, provenance or meaning | Answer one short question |
| Duplicate source | Existing staged source and first capture time are shown. No second source is created | **View saved source** |
| Processing delayed but source safe | Original remains visible with **Saved. Still processing** and no inferred meaning | **Done** |
| Ready to use in Claude | Reviewed cut shows purpose, included sources, exclusion, audience, freshness and expiry | **Use this context** |
| Operator-private content excluded | Private item remains outside the opening and is listed under **Stays in CTRL** | **Use this context** |
| Copied task starter and Claude unavailable fallback | Starter contents are previewed and confirmed as containing no Brain material | **Copy task starter** |
| Returned Claude result in staging | Amber receipt says Claude suggestion, private staging, source not truth | **Keep in staging** |
| Wrong customer | Aperture closes before sharing, names the mismatch and preserves inbound original where applicable | **Return to Maya** or **Keep with active customer**, never both in one state |
| Expired context | Aperture closes, Claude receives nothing and the previous cut remains inspectable in CTRL | **Make a fresh cut** |

## 9. Failure and recovery

Failure closes the aperture before it widens authority.

- **Wrong customer before capture:** show both customer names in plain text, preserve the original fragment locally in the receipt and ask one routing question. Do not guess.
- **Wrong customer before Claude handoff:** close the outward aperture and block the context reference. The one recovery action returns to the customer named by the cut.
- **Expired context:** the read-only path returns no Brain contents. CTRL shows the expired audience, purpose and time, then offers **Make a fresh cut**.
- **Duplicate:** preserve one source record and point to it. Do not make the user resolve a false conflict.
- **Delayed processing:** preserve the original input and its staging receipt. Do not invent a summary while processing is incomplete.
- **Connector unavailable:** keep the reviewed cut unchanged and offer the task starter. Do not claim Claude is ready.
- **Clipboard denied:** preserve the starter on screen and offer **Select task starter** as the one recovery action.
- **File or voice unsupported:** preserve the selected original where the browser permits and state exactly what was not read or transcribed.
- **Return provenance missing:** keep the result as an unattributed local draft, do not label it Claude output and ask one provenance question before staging it as a source.

No failure deletes the original input, silently changes customer, falls back to whole-Brain export or promotes returned work.

## 10. Hard-failure check

| Hard failure | How this spine prevents it |
|---|---|
| Whole-Brain or vague automatic context sharing | The aperture begins from the named Decision Bench purpose, includes only fixture-backed connected items and has no select-all path. Every inclusion is visible before action. |
| Trusted-memory write-back | Capture and return end in private staging. The first surface contains no control that can accept staged material into durable Brain truth. |
| Unclear active customer | Maya Chen, Aperture House and the private audience stay fixed before capture, review, handoff and return. |
| Technical setup as the daily experience | Daily interaction is paste, purpose, visible cut and receipt. Connector, capsule and identifier detail is hidden from the main path. |
| More than one primary ask per state | Each state has one question or one primary action. Alternate capture media answer the same ask and do not create parallel workflows. |
| Generic upload or chat UI | The governing action is a purpose-shaped cut through typed Brain relationships. Capture, handoff and return share the same aperture and trust boundary. There is no transcript or generic file queue. |
| Active-looking control that cannot work in the proof | The proof renders only browser-backed capture controls and deterministic fixture interactions. Connector-dependent actions appear only when the read-only substrate acknowledges availability. Otherwise the working copy fallback is primary. |
| Unsupported product claim | All customer meaning, sources and relationships resolve to the Maya fixture. Synthetic success is labelled interaction proof only. No diagnostic quality, automatic understanding or customer outcome is claimed. |

## 11. Feasibility with the current substrate

The concept needs no new production capability.

### Existing React Decision Bench

The aperture is a contextual exchange state attached to the current decision and focused Living Brain route. It does not replace the approved Bench, navigation or evidence model. The Bench already supplies the active customer, decision, current read, open question, evidence and typed directed relationships required to propose the first cut.

### Synthetic fixture

The proof binds to the named Maya Chen scenario and uses deterministic fixture references for the decision, current supported read, open question, at least three sources, private exclusion and provenance roles. Context selection is a local array of fixture identifiers. No embedding, retrieval model, inference, confidence score or whole-Brain serialization is needed.

The proof may use a fixed synthetic clock to calculate and display an exact expiry. It must label that as proof state, not as evidence of a deployed security control.

### Existing dialogs

The ready, one-question, inspect-detail and failure closures can use the existing dialog substrate, but the aperture itself remains anchored to the Decision Bench. It must not become a sequence of setup modals. Each dialog receives one purpose, one focus target and one return route to the same aperture state.

### Read-only MCP substrate

The outward handoff creates a purpose-bound, expiring reference to the selected fixture identifiers. The connector can read that reference and its allowed synthetic items. It cannot write a Claude result back to the Brain. Return therefore remains a deliberate paste or explicit synthetic receive action into local private staging.

The copied task starter contains only a short instruction, the active customer label and the opaque reference. Manual export is separate, previews the exact content and occurs only after explicit choice.

### Local proof state

React state can represent aperture direction, active step, selected fixture identifiers, excluded identifiers, original captured input, receipt status, ambiguity choice, expiry, connector availability and returned staged source. Browser clipboard, file input, drag and drop and optional MediaRecorder cover the visible controls. Unsupported browser capability results in an honest unavailable state, not a dead affordance.

No account, connector installation, schema write, live Claude request, automatic prompt injection, customer sharing or trusted-memory transition is required.

## 12. Why this is not a familiar shell

It is not an upload modal because receiving a file is only one inward crossing. The product-specific work is the immediate private staging receipt, the visible distinction between understood and not assumed and the same boundary carrying purpose-bound context outward.

It is not a chat composer because there is no transcript, assistant persona or turn-taking home. Claude work happens in Claude. CTRL prepares and governs the context, then receives the result as a source.

It is not a settings or permission screen because the user never administers scopes, integrations, tokens or policy tables. The aperture answers a concrete work question: **What does Claude need for this task, and what stays here?**

It is not a generic integration dashboard because one active customer and one consequential decision own the surface. The Living Brain remains the source of the cut and the Decision Bench remains the place where the returned work becomes useful.

## 13. Strongest failure risk and kill test

The strongest risk is that the aperture becomes an attractive selection visual that still makes Krish manage context item by item. If he must understand the Brain's structure, tune a long inclusion list or reason about permissions before using Claude, the concept has become administration.

The proof must therefore begin with a useful minimum cut derived deterministically from the current decision, current read, open question and directly linked evidence. Manual adjustment is an exception. The default should usually require one review and one action.

Kill the spine if a cold user cannot answer these questions within ten seconds:

1. Which customer am I working on?
2. What will Claude see?
3. What stays private?
4. What do I do next?

Also kill it if the representative task needs whole-Brain selection, more than one clarification, an inactive connector control, a hidden clipboard payload or any route that turns Claude output into trusted memory.

## 14. Conceptual spine summary

**Sequencing:** capture inward, receive, set purpose, review the narrowed cut, use in Claude, return through the same boundary.  
**User agency:** accept or adjust a small proposed cut, never administer the whole Brain.  
**Primary interaction:** purpose-led aperture over fixture-backed Brain objects.  
**Information structure:** visible Brain field, bounded inclusion opening and adjacent state receipt.  
**State model:** inward capture, private staging, outward review, read-only availability, expiry and attributed return staging.

If selected, the next action is one synthetic rendered proof of this spine inside the approved Decision Bench. No implementation should begin before the concept is judged and the rendered surface receives explicit founder approval.
