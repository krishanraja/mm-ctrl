| Sequencing | Compare, inspect, decide | Select commitment level, read response, act or step back | Follow the active route, locate its break, inspect |

Every pair differs on all five recorded axes. Styling and copy are not carrying the distinction. The set passes the minimum diversity threshold.

## Fixture feasibility

### Concept A

The fixture directly supplies a current intervention, current synthesis, discarded interpretation, three linked sources, five typed relationships, an open gap, a next move, alternatives, private guidance and a customer projection. Selection, pinning, filtering and evidence inspection can remain local view state. The concept does not require a model call or durable write.

The proposed desktop arithmetic needs correction before rendering. A 72 pixel rail plus two 24 pixel gutters leaves 1320 pixels, not the 1344 pixels allocated by the concept. This is a frame-level correction, not a product limitation.

### Concept B

The fixture can populate the named evidence, uncertainty, change and current-move instruments. The commitment lever can be implemented as local view state.

The fixture does not contain decision thresholds or clearance rules for Understand, Test and Commit. The concept recognises this and proposes honest fallback language, but the lever could still imply a certainty model the product does not possess. Any synthesis must use the control to change the question being asked of the evidence, not to calculate permission.

### Concept C

The fixture directly supplies ten Brain items, five typed directional relationships, provenance, history and a named gap. A deterministic local relationship field and attention lens are feasible.

The fixture does not mark relationship-level uncertainty. `GAP-014` is a separate knowledge gap rather than a typed edge. A render must attach the gap to the relevant route as an explicit linked annotation, not pretend the relationship schema already contains uncertainty. Selecting a gap from graph shape alone would be an unsupported inference.

## Pre-judge conclusion

All three concepts are feasible as local proofs after the named guardrails. Concept A has a geometry correction. Concept B has a false-clearance risk. Concept C has an uncertainty-schema risk. These remain judge inputs rather than a winner decision.
