# G22 concept spine: The Margin

**Revision:** C1

**Status:** Independent concept candidate, ready for synthesis and visual design

## The call

Build CTRL as a quiet margin beside the work. Most of the time the margin is empty. When the Brain can show that a live choice may change because of what it knows about Maya, one prepared note appears. It contains the tension, the specific reason it matters to Maya, and one question whose answer changes the route.

The companion feeling comes from behaviour: preparation, restraint, specificity and continuity. It does not come from an avatar, a name, typing dots, chat bubbles or claims that CTRL thinks like a person.

The sharper alternative would make CTRL continuously visible as a work partner. Reject that route for this proof. Continuous presence creates pressure to fill space, which quickly becomes a feed or a chat surface. The empty margin is part of the product.

## State of use and governing rule

- **Primary user:** the advisor, on a wide laptop between customer sessions.
- **Secondary user:** Maya, on a narrow phone for one deliberately timed choice.
- **Cognitive state:** busy, accountable and unwilling to learn a new system before making the call.
- **Action:** understand why this issue deserves attention, answer one route-changing question, make the decision in the approved instrument, govern the learning, and later see that the learning changed useful work.
- **Governing rule:** CTRL may interrupt only when a current source, a Maya-specific criterion and a consequential choice form one traceable chain.
- **Materiality:** new whole-product interaction spine. It requires visual approval before implementation.
- **Data truth:** every claim carries an origin and standing. A provisional interpretation never appears as an accepted Maya belief.

## Product metaphor

The surface behaves like a well-prepared colleague who has written one note in the margin of the exact page under discussion, then stayed silent. The product never says this metaphor aloud.

Three rules make the metaphor operational:

1. **One note, not a queue.** Only the highest-value earned intervention can occupy the margin. There is no inbox, unread count or backlog.
2. **The note is attached to work.** It always names the source object, subject, purpose and audience. It cannot float free as generic advice.
3. **A later note can cite an earlier call.** Continuity is shown by a specific accepted criterion changing a later preparation, not by CTRL saying it remembers Maya.

## Product shell and information structure

The shell stays stable while one central work region changes.

### Fixed shell

- Compact top line: `Aperture House / Maya Chen / 30-day proof`.
- A quiet Brain standing control: `Brain current`, `Brain has a stale source`, or `Brain needs evidence`. This appears once and only when it affects the current choice.
- Two secondary utilities: `Inspect Brain` and `Take a copy`.
- No global activity feed, notification bell, assistant portrait or conversation history.

### Changing work region

The work region has two parts that read as one sheet:

- **The page:** the current work object, such as the marketing operating plan, Decision Table, proposed learning, release check or later preparation.
- **The margin:** a narrow reserved band at the right on desktop and a compact edge tab at the bottom on mobile. It is blank unless an intervention has earned attention.

The page never competes with the note. Opening the margin note dims supporting detail and brings the note into the reading line. The result of every action replaces or updates the note in place.

### Four disclosure levels

1. **Quiet edge:** no intervention, or one short earned marker.
2. **Prepared note:** tension, Maya-specific reason and one question.
3. **Decision work:** the approved R4 Decision Table, entered only after the question establishes the lens.
4. **Proof of continuity:** the accepted learning, its source chain and the later output it changed.

Source detail is always available through a small labelled control beside the claim. It opens an inline provenance drawer within the natural page flow, never a nested scrolling card.

## Primary interaction

The primary interaction is pulling one prepared note from the margin onto the work page.

Closed note copy:

> One thing may change this call.

Open note copy:

> This plan changes eight roles. Its scorecard still rewards volume.

Origin labels sit directly below each sentence:

> Company work

> Maya, accepted standard

The Maya-specific line follows:

> Maya's standard is clarity without killing surprise. The Brain has no accepted evidence that this standard can travel without her final rescue.

The single question:

> Before this moves, what must the plan prove?

Answer chips:

- `The quality bar can travel`
- `The roles really change`
- `The budget holds`

The representative path selects `The quality bar can travel`. That answer sets `quality_transfer` as the active decision lens. The result appears below the chips:

> The table will test whether quality can travel without making Maya the final rescue.

The next action, `Open the decision table`, appears only after the answer. There is no open-ended reply box. Optional voice input is limited to `Name another proof`, then transcribed into a short answer that Maya confirms before use.

## Golden path in eight primary actions

The complete proof uses eight primary actions. Secondary source inspection and export are available but do not advance the decision path.

| Action | Actor and viewport | State change | Visible result at the point of action |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. `Open note` | Advisor, desktop | `READY` to `NOTE_OPEN` | The evidence-backed tension and why it matters to Maya appear. |
| 2. `The quality bar can travel` | Maya, phone | `ASKING` to `LENS_SET` | The selected lens appears below the chips. |
| 3. `Open the decision table` | Advisor, desktop | `LENS_SET` to `TABLE_OPEN` | The approved R4 Decision Table fills the work region with the lens attached. |
| 4. `Set working call` | Maya or advisor beside Maya, desktop | `TABLE_OPEN` to `CALL_SET` | The chosen route becomes the working call, not a released decision. |
| 5. `Not quite` | Maya, phone | `LEARNING_PROPOSED` to `CORRECTING` | A small set of boundary corrections appears in place. |
| 6. `Launch work only` | Maya, phone | `CORRECTING` to `LEARNING_ACCEPTED` | The corrected learning appears with `Accepted by Maya`. |
| 7. `Approve for release` | Maya, desktop or phone | `RELEASE_CHECK` to `RELEASE_APPROVED` | The local proof records Maya's release decision and states `Nothing was sent`. |
| 8. `Show what changed` | Advisor, later desktop return | `LATER_READY` to `LATER_PROOF` | The earlier accepted learning and the exact changed part of the new preparation appear together. |

`Approve for release` only changes local deterministic state. It does not send, publish or write to a database.

## The Decision Table as an earned deeper state

The approved R4 Decision Table remains intact. This concept does not change its rows, controls, density, scoring, hierarchy or visual system.

CTRL earns entry to it in three steps:

1. The prepared note shows the current tension.
2. Maya's answer sets one active lens.
3. `Open the decision table` replaces the note page with the existing R4 instrument.

The concept adds only a thin context frame outside the approved organ:

- Above: `Decision lens: can Maya's quality bar travel?`
- Beside any imported evidence: origin and standing labels supplied by the existing table contract.
- Below, after the working call: `What CTRL may learn from this`.

The table is never the home screen. Closing it returns to the same note at its current state. The user does not lose a selection.

## Proposed learning, correction and acceptance

After the working call, CTRL proposes one learning:

> Proposed: Maya keeps the final review for all marketing work.

Controls:

- `Accept`
- `Not quite`
- `Reject`

The representative path chooses `Not quite`, then `Launch work only`.

The repaired statement appears in the same place:

> Accepted: Maya keeps the final call on launch work. Weekly work can use the agreed quality check.

Directly below it, a compact history line remains available:

> Corrected from: final review for all marketing work.

The superseded interpretation remains in the event history with its sources and correction reason. It is excluded from current inference and downstream outputs. Any staged downstream object that depended on it receives a new version linked to the correction event.

## Later continuity proof

On the later return, a new synthetic launch preparation reaches the Brain. CTRL does not announce a memory. It changes the preparation and marks the changed line with the same margin stitch used earlier.

Visible copy:

> This is launch work. Maya keeps the final call.

> Added because Maya corrected the working rule on 18 September.

`Show what changed` reveals a compact before and after:

| Before the accepted learning | After the accepted learning |
|---|---|
| Final approval assigned to the marketing lead. | Final approval assigned to Maya for launch work. |
| Weekly work and launch work treated alike. | Weekly work uses the agreed quality check. Launch work keeps Maya's final call. |

This is the proof of compounding. One earlier correction improves a later preparation without another interview, while the leader still owns the final call.

## Meaningful visual system

### The margin stitch

A thin vertical stitch runs beside the current intervention. It is not a progress bar.

- One short segment equals one cited source record.
- A solid segment means accepted evidence.
- A hollow segment means a provisional CTRL interpretation.
- A broken segment means stale evidence that cannot carry the call.
- A short cross mark means rejected learning retained only in history.
- A small joined branch means a correction superseded an earlier interpretation.

Selecting a segment expands the exact source label and date beside the claim it supports. The line never moves without a data change.

### State-responsive motion

- The closed note enters once with a 160 to 220 millisecond lateral reveal, as if the margin widened.
- Opening it extends source segments to their claims.
- Acceptance turns a hollow segment solid.
- Correction branches the segment, keeps the old endpoint faint and joins the new endpoint.
- Rejection shortens the active stitch and leaves a cross only in history.
- Reduced-motion mode uses immediate state replacement and unchanged spatial semantics.

No pulse runs in the quiet state. Ambient does not mean always moving.

### Colour and type

Use a cool technical ground, paper-like raised surface and one amber signal colour. Amber means new or changed. Standing, rejection and staleness use line form, labels and weight rather than extra decorative colours. Monospace supports sources, dates, versions and control labels. A highly legible text face carries the note. Type never impersonates handwriting.

### Living Brain projection

`Inspect Brain` opens a bounded projection of the current chain, not a free-roaming graph:

`Sources -> claims -> accepted criteria -> affected outputs`

Each visible object is a real fixture record. Each connector is a stored citation, derivation or correction link. No unconnected node appears. Users can select an object to read its source, standing, evidence, uncertainty, audience, version and history. They cannot drag nodes, zoom through a decorative network or mistake layout proximity for meaning.

Personal records and company-owned records occupy labelled lanes. A connector may cross lanes only when the derived object stores the permitted source reference and audience. The projection is a view over the same governed objects used by the note and export.

### Deterministic local event log

Every primary action appends one local event with `event_id`, `event_type`, `actor`, `object_id`, `from_version`, `to_version`, `occurred_at` and `synthetic: true`. Replaying the log must reproduce the visible state. Correction never mutates an earlier event.

### Portable ZIP

`Take a copy` creates a client-side ZIP with no network request:

```text
aperture-house-brain/
  README.md
  manifest.json
  sources/index.json
  brain/claims.jsonl
  brain/accepted-criteria.jsonl
  decisions/decision-01.json
  history/events.jsonl
  outputs/later-prep-01.md
```

The manifest states that the data is synthetic, names the subject and owner lanes, records the schema version and export time, and lists every included file. The output excludes untouched default choices and includes complete option text plus Maya's recorded answer and correction.

## State model

```text
QUIET
  -> READY
  -> NOTE_OPEN
  -> ASKING
  -> LENS_SET
  -> TABLE_OPEN
  -> CALL_SET
  -> LEARNING_PROPOSED
     -> LEARNING_ACCEPTED
     -> CORRECTING -> LEARNING_ACCEPTED
     -> LEARNING_REJECTED
  -> RELEASE_CHECK
  -> RELEASE_APPROVED
  -> LATER_READY
  -> LATER_PROOF
```

Orthogonal evidence states can block or qualify the path:

- `EVIDENCE_SPARSE` blocks `READY`.
- `EVIDENCE_STALE_NONCRITICAL` permits a note only when the stale record is visibly excluded.
- `EVIDENCE_STALE_CRITICAL` blocks the decision recommendation and returns the margin to a source repair state.
- `LOCAL_SAVE_FAILED` preserves the current input in memory, offers retry and download, and never claims acceptance or release.

The product stores the current object ID and event cursor. Refresh, viewport change and a return on the same browser restore the exact point without repeating the question.

## Desktop choreography

At wide width, the work sheet occupies roughly three quarters of the region and the margin the remaining quarter. The margin does not become a second dashboard column. It has room for one note only.

1. Quiet shell opens on the current work object.
2. An earned margin marker appears aligned to the sentence or decision it concerns.
3. Opening it widens the margin and compresses supporting page detail without covering the work.
4. The selected answer returns its effect directly below the chips.
5. The Decision Table then replaces the full work region. The context frame stays compact.
6. Proposed learning returns as a short governed object, not a modal.
7. Later proof uses a two-column before and after only on desktop.

The page owns vertical scrolling. No panel or card has its own scrollbar. Keyboard focus follows the visual sequence and returns to the invoking control when a detail closes.

## Mobile choreography

On a narrow phone, the margin becomes a labelled bottom-edge tab. It never floats as a chat bubble.

- Quiet: the work title and `Nothing needs Maya today.` fit above the fold.
- Ready: one tab reads `One thing may change this call`.
- Open: one claim per short block, then one question. Source labels remain visible but compact.
- Answer: large full-width chips with at least one clear line of separation. The selected result appears immediately beneath them.
- Decision Table: use the approved R4 mobile behaviour unchanged. This concept adds only the one-line decision lens above it.
- Correction: one screen asks `Where does Maya keep the final call?` with `Launch work only`, `All marketing work`, and `Name another boundary`.
- Later proof: show the changed line first, then `Show earlier version`. Do not force a side-by-side comparison onto a phone.

Primary actions remain reachable above the safe area when the virtual keyboard opens. Voice has a text and tap alternative. Rotation, zoom and browser chrome may change height without hiding the current question or action.

## Sparse, stale, correction and rejection handling

### Quiet or no earned interruption

Visible copy:

> Nothing needs Maya today.

The margin remains blank. There is no empty card, disabled suggestion list or invitation to ask CTRL anything.

### Sparse evidence

CTRL does not manufacture a tension from one source. The advisor sees:

> Not enough evidence to interrupt.

`Review sources` shows which required parts are missing: current work, Maya-specific criterion or consequential choice. The state offers no recommendation.

### Stale evidence

If the scorecard is beyond `review_due_at`, its stitch breaks and the note says:

> The scorecard is out of date. CTRL has not used it for this call.

If removing it leaves enough evidence, the intervention continues with a narrower claim. If it is required, the note collapses to sparse evidence. The deterministic proof swaps between bundled stale and current fixtures through its test-state switcher, never by pretending to refresh a live source.

### Correction

`Not quite` always sits beside `Accept`. Correction offers the smallest relevant boundaries first and a voice or text fallback. It creates a new version, preserves the old wording and repairs every staged dependent object. Released historical outputs remain historically accurate and gain a `superseded` link rather than silent rewriting.

### Rejection

`Reject` removes the learning from current Brain use. The history retains the exact rejected wording, source chain, actor, time and optional reason. CTRL cannot propose the same claim again unless a new cited source changes the evidence set. The user sees:

> Rejected. This will not shape later work.

No apology, counterargument or follow-up prompt appears.

## Why this is not a chat wrapper

- There is no composer, transcript, assistant message, user message, avatar, typing state or conversational memory theatre.
- The user acts on governed objects: a source-backed tension, one structured question, a decision, a learning proposal, a correction and a release state.
- Responses change typed fields and downstream versions. They do not add bubbles to a conversation.
- Optional voice fills one bounded field and requires confirmation. It does not open an unbounded dialogue.
- CTRL's intelligence is visible in what it prepared, excluded and later changed, not in fluent prose volume.

## Why this is not a notification feed

- The margin can hold one earned intervention and no backlog.
- There are no unread counts, chronological cards, priority badges, infinite scroll or engagement loops.
- Quiet is a successful product state.
- History lives in the governed Brain and is opened for provenance, not consumption.
- The later return is triggered by changed work and an accepted criterion, not by the age of a notification.

## Feasibility boundary

The first proof can be implemented as one deterministic finite state machine over fixture JSON and an append-only browser-local event log. All transitions are local. The R4 Decision Table is mounted unchanged as the `TABLE_OPEN` state. The Brain view reads the same records and links used by the margin note. ZIP generation occurs in the browser.

No state requires a model call, database, account, message send, external research request, GitHub connection or production navigation change. Test fixtures select representative, quiet, sparse, stale, corrected, rejected and later-return conditions without exposing a fake product control.

## Acceptance checks for a future rendered proof

- A first-time viewer can state, without a tour, why CTRL interrupted and why the issue is specific to Maya.
- The representative path needs no more than eight primary actions.
- The Decision Table appears only after the lens-setting answer and remains visually unchanged inside its frame.
- Every visible claim exposes origin and standing in one action or less.
- Quiet, sparse and stale states make no recommendation they cannot support.
- Correction produces a new accepted version, preserves history and changes the staged later preparation.
- Rejection prevents the claim from shaping the later preparation.
- The later proof names the accepted rule and shows the exact changed output line.
- Desktop and phone preserve the same semantic state, with one immediate mobile action.
- Keyboard, touch, voice fallback, zoom, reduced motion and input persistence have observable test cases.
- The downloaded ZIP is self-describing and contains no untouched defaults.
- No surface resembles a chat, notification feed, dashboard, tour or graph explorer.

## Residual risk and flip condition

The risk is that the margin metaphor feels too passive in a demonstration and the founder misses the prepared note. Do not solve that with badges, pulses or a feed. First test whether the single lateral reveal plus plain copy is noticed in five seconds on desktop and phone. If it is missed in both contexts, enlarge the reserved margin and strengthen the note's spatial contrast while preserving one-note restraint. If users notice it but cannot explain why it matters, the failure is evidence hierarchy, not salience.

The concept succeeds when restraint itself proves intelligence: CTRL knows when to prepare, when to ask, when to stay quiet and exactly what an earlier correction changes next.
