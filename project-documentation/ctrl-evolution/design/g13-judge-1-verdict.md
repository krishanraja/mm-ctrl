## Criterion findings

### Candidate Z

1. **Product and value are understandable: holds strongly.** Section 2 opens on the owned call and reveals the aim and standard that were recorded with it. The sequence answers what the Brain knows and why it matters before asking the user to explore the system.
2. **Portrait and Living Map feel like one Brain: holds, with a gap.** Section 7 preserves wording, kind marks, change state, and selection when entering the map. The map nevertheless arrives as a later mode, so the physical continuity is described more than demonstrated.
3. **Correction and repair are trustworthy: holds strongly.** Section 8 uses **Before** and **From now on**, preserves the prior meaning with history, rebuilds only replaceable projections, and names the board brief and shared release as review-required rather than rewritten.
4. **Radically simple under time pressure: holds.** The first 30 seconds are one decision and one next action at a time. The user reaches correction without a dashboard, graph controls, or chat container.
5. **Immersive, alive, and distinctive: holds.** The fixed Now marker, bounded wake, and one-time forward pulse make version change perceptible without ambient motion. The later map still has to prove that it is more than a secondary visualization.
6. **Visual semantics cannot overclaim: holds provisionally.** Section 2 carefully labels the beats as recorded with the decision, and section 11 says causal verbs require a matching current relationship. However, section 4 calls the interaction a reveal of the next recorded **cause or consequence**. That phrase is not supported by LB01 and must not survive into the proof.
7. **Sparse, dense, and adverse states: holds.** Sections 6 and 9 cover private rejection, proposed, disputed, superseded, loading, stale, recoverable error, sparse use, long labels, and bounded 18/24 semantic zoom.
8. **Fixture feasibility: holds.** The concept explicitly maps LB01 to the opening accepted state, LB04 to correction and repair, and the remaining fixtures to adverse and scale states without requiring production implementation.

## Hard failures and invariant regressions

- **Candidate Y has a hard failure.** It draws a `supports` relationship between the standard and the decision that the fixture does not contain. This regresses the rule that every visible semantic relationship must be current, typed, evidence-bearing, and attached to the exact supplied endpoints. It is therefore ineligible despite its strong spatial concept.
- **Candidate X has no identified hard failure or invariant regression.**
- **Candidate Z has no definitive invariant regression on paper, but its own phrase `recorded cause or consequence` is unsafe.** The visible copy elsewhere limits the wake to recorded co-presence and the actual standard-to-aim relationship. The proof must remove causal framing and must be rejected if users infer an unsupported explanation of why the decision was made.
- None of the candidates crosses the production authority boundary. All remain concept-only, self-contained proof directions.

## Is any candidate disguised repetition of rejected round 1?

**No.** All three retain necessary brief-mandated beats such as evidence, correction, and repair, but none repeats the rejected fold, unfolding-map, atlas, paper, or open-and-trace structure across all load-bearing axes.

- Candidate X uses a stable focal object with mutually exclusive lenses. Its selected-item continuity resembles a useful quality from round 1, but the governing interaction and state model are fixed-object inspection, not unfolding.
- Candidate Y is genuinely spatial-first. It starts inside the map and makes focus hopping and free navigation primary.
- Candidate Z is the closest superficial match because it uses a guided reveal before correction. It is not disguised repetition because the ordering is temporal, the decision remains at Now, past wording stays attached to prior use, and the map follows the temporal value moment rather than unfolding from a portrait.

Shared correction steps do not constitute conceptual sameness because the brief requires them.

## Useful round 1 strengths to restore without copying it

No useful strength was entirely lost across the set, but three were weakened and should be deliberately retained in synthesis:

1. **Literal continuity of the selected object.** Round 1 made the same statement visibly travel from portrait into map. Candidate X preserves this best. Candidate Z should carry the exact decision or standard object into the wider map, not merely recreate it with matching style.
2. **One relationship trace at a time.** Round 1 kept the active connection, explanation, and evidence tightly coupled. Candidate Z's later map should foreground one labelled relationship and place its evidence action at that connection, without restoring a fold or atlas metaphor.
3. **Selective repair motion.** Round 1 clearly withdrew only the two affected routes and left the unaffected tension route still. The winner should retain that disciplined contrast because it turns repair into visible proof rather than decoration.

Do not restore the fold, paper, atlas, flap, cover, route-tracing sequence, or opening portrait choreography. Those were the source of round 1 sameness.

## Winner and bounded synthesis

**Select Candidate Z for the functional proof, conditionally.** It best matches the two-minute use case because it begins with an owned consequential decision, makes the correction's time boundary intuitive, and gives the review-required consequences real meaning. Its 91 is the highest weighted score and it has no definitive hard failure as specified, but the causal-comprehension test is a blocking condition for advancing it.

Borrow only these bounded elements:

- From Candidate X, hold the selected standard at one stable visual point during source inspection, before/from-now-on comparison, and correction confirmation. Do not import the four-lens architecture.
- From Candidate Y, use a strong static focus outline plus one explicit Find or jump control after the Living Map opens. Do not import map-first sequencing, the unsupported decision connection, or a cluster of pan and zoom controls into the mobile hero.
- From round 1, use one labelled relationship trace at a time and selective redraw of only affected relationships. Do not import any fold, atlas, paper, or open-and-trace metaphor.

## Preserved dissent

The strongest dissenting case is Candidate X. It is the most contract-safe concept, makes portrait/map parity most perceptible, and avoids Candidate Z's causal-ordering risk. If cold users interpret Candidate Z as claiming that CTRL knows why the decision was made, Candidate X should replace it rather than adding explanatory copy or a legend.

Candidate Y supplies the strongest immersive field, and after a fresh correction it could be worth testing as a separate spatial alternative. It cannot be selected or silently repaired in synthesis because the submitted concept contains an unsupported semantic relationship.

## Strongest failure risk and flip rule

Candidate Z's strongest risk is **causal overreading**. A polished sequence from pressure to aim to standard to call can feel like proof of why the user decided, while LB01 proves only recorded evidence and a typed `supports` relationship between the standard and the aim.

The functional proof should ask a cold user what the sequence claims. If they say, in substance, **CTRL knows why I made that decision**, the concept fails. The acceptable reading is **these were recorded with the decision, and this one supported relationship is evidenced**. On failure, switch to Candidate X. Do not try to rescue the temporal spine with heavier explanatory furniture.

## Owner decision

Advance Candidate Z to one self-contained functional proof only after removing the phrase **recorded cause or consequence** from the interaction definition and encoding recorded co-presence separately from typed semantic relationships. Candidate Y is rejected as submitted. Candidate X remains the named fallback. This verdict is advisory and does not approve implementation beyond the already-authorised proof boundary.
