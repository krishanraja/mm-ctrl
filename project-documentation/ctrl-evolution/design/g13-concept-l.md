# Concept L: The Fixed Point

## 1. Governing interaction metaphor

The Brain behaves like an optical inspection stage. One piece of judgement is held at a fixed focal point while the user taps between four labelled lenses: **Portrait**, **Connections**, **Sources**, and **Consequences**. The judgement stays in the same position, at the same scale, with the same wording. Only the field around it changes.

This makes the object of scrutiny feel stable and owned. Portrait answers, "Where does this sit in how I think?" Connections answers, "What does this currently support or qualify?" Sources answers, "What did this understanding come from?" Consequences answers, "Where is it being used, and what would change if I corrected it?"

The Connections lens is the Living Map at the scale of the selected judgement. It is not a separate destination and does not ask the user to open, pan, or trace a diagram. A lens change reveals only current, typed, evidence-bearing relationships around the fixed point.

## 2. Exact first 30-second sequence

**0 to 3 seconds:** The screen resolves directly to a single focal statement under the quiet label **How I judge**, scoped by **Marketing strategy · Private to you**:

> Before consequential work ships, the human owns the final judgement and finish.

The only prominent prompt is **Does this still sound right?** with two actions: **Yes** and **Refine**. The four lens labels sit within thumb reach below the fixed statement. Portrait is active.

**3 to 9 seconds:** The Portrait field shows the two nearest human meanings without becoming a card grid: the current aim, **Shape an AI-native category**, and the owned call, **Redesign the work before judging the people**. Their placement says only that they share this projection, not that proximity is semantic.

**9 to 15 seconds:** The user taps **Connections**. The focal statement does not move. Two relationship phrases appear, each attached by a restrained line to its other current item: **supports the category aim** and **is qualified by distinctive human voice**. Each phrase is the interaction target, so the user never has to decode line style.

**15 to 21 seconds:** The user taps **supports the category aim**. A single sentence replaces the short label at the point of the connection: **Human release judgement protects the quality of AI-native category work.** A small **See basis** action appears beside it.

**21 to 26 seconds:** The user taps **Sources**. The connection clears, while the focal statement remains. The source field shows **You said this · Interview · 7 Sep**, the exact supporting assertion, and **View in context**. There is no confidence score standing in for provenance.

**26 to 30 seconds:** The user taps **Refine**. The surrounding field becomes a sentence editor while the original remains visibly anchored above it. The first question is concrete: **What is too broad?** The interface offers **Consequential work** as a selected phrase and accepts ordinary typed or spoken language. At 30 seconds the user is already correcting the meaning, not administering memory.

## 3. Where the user has agency

The user controls five consequential choices:

1. Keep the focal judgement or select a different accepted item from the Portrait lens.
2. Switch lenses by tap, keyboard focus, or arrow keys. Swiping may be a convenience, never the only route.
3. Select a relationship phrase to reveal its plain-language meaning and evidence.
4. Refine, dispute, or retire the focal judgement in ordinary language, with the resulting scope and consequences shown before commitment.
5. Open each review-required output from the repair receipt and decide what, if anything, should be changed.

CTRL chooses the first focal item deterministically from the current task context and explains **Why this is in focus** on request. It does not change focus through ambient motion. Focus changes only through an explicit user selection or a newly applied correction to that same item.

## 4. Primary interaction

The primary interaction is **change the lens, not the object**. Four direct tap targets exchange the surrounding projection while preserving the fixed judgement. There is no freeform canvas manipulation, drag dependency, command composer, or default conversation.

Each lens permits one next action:

- **Portrait:** choose another accepted judgement.
- **Connections:** inspect one named relationship.
- **Sources:** inspect the basis in context.
- **Consequences:** refine the judgement or review an affected use.

On mobile, the focal statement and lens rail remain visible while the single page scrolls. On desktop, the same structure gains space but not additional panels or simultaneous modes. Focus order follows focal statement, lens rail, lens content, action.

## 5. Information structure

The surface has four persistent layers:

1. **Scope line:** current task context, audience boundary, and as-of state in human language.
2. **Fixed point:** item kind, exact current meaning, current standing, and one action, usually **Refine**. Version detail is available through **Previous meaning**, not exposed as system syntax.
3. **Lens field:** one of four mutually exclusive projections around the fixed point.
4. **Action edge:** the one next action warranted by the active lens, positioned beside the evidence, relationship, or consequence it affects.

Portrait uses the five human meanings: What matters, How I judge, My calls, Unresolved, and What changed. It shows at most twelve accepted high-value items and decisions, but only the selected item occupies the fixed point. Connections shows the orientation or neighbourhood of the Living Map. Sources shows assertions and their source envelopes. Consequences shows current dependency uses, prior change history, and repair receipts.

Every visible semantic connection contains its relationship verb and a plain-language explanation. Visual lines reinforce that statement but never replace it. Spatial distance has no stated meaning. Node size may only reflect relevance to the current projection.

## 6. State model

| State | Fixed point | Lens behaviour | Available action |
|---|---|---|---|
| Accepted current | Stable, full treatment | Appears in Portrait and Connections when eligible | Inspect or refine |
| Proposed | Never occupies the accepted fixed point | Absent from accepted Portrait and Connections; may appear in a separately labelled **For your review** state | Accept, revise, or reject |
| Disputed or superseded | Absent from current steering views | Available only through prior meaning or change history where lawful | Inspect history |
| Correction being composed | Original remains fixed above the draft | Consequences previews projection rebuilds and review-required outputs | Apply correction or cancel |
| Repairing | Newly accepted wording occupies the fixed point | Lenses are temporarily read-only and show named work in progress | Wait or leave safely |
| Repaired | Corrected wording remains fixed | Portrait and Connections show the new canonical state; Consequences shows terminal receipts | Review flagged outputs |
| Private audience rejected | No item wording, count, or relationship leaks | Lenses are unavailable | Return or use an authorised account |
| Loading | Reserved focal geometry without simulated content | Lens labels are inert until eligibility is known | Cancel or leave |
| Stale | Last verified projection is visibly time-stamped; no unverified link is added | Inspection remains possible, correction is held until current state is verified | Refresh |
| Recoverable error | Last verified safe state remains if available, otherwise no Brain content appears | Failed lens names the unavailable operation | Retry |

Reduced-motion mode replaces the brief lens transitions with immediate state changes. Visible focus, 44-pixel minimum targets, text alternatives for every visual relationship, and ordinary typing remain present throughout.

## 7. How Portrait and Living Map remain visibly one Brain

The selected item's wording, item-kind label, audience state, and screen position are identical in Portrait and Connections. Switching to Connections does not navigate away or recreate the item as a differently styled node. The surrounding Portrait meanings recede and the supported relationship field arrives around the same fixed point.

This continuity is backed by canonical-reference parity. In LB01, the focal standard, aim, voice preference, tension, context, and decision are drawn from the same eligible versions in both projections. The interface does not normally expose identifiers, but **Why this is in focus** can reveal a compact verification line such as **Same current understanding in Portrait and Connections · verified now**.

Only semantic relationships appear as selectable connections. Invisible layout tethers may steady the composition but receive no line, label, hit target, or inspector entry. A relationship is understandable through its verb and explanation without a legend. The proposed inferred brainstorm pattern is absent from the accepted field.

## 8. The correction and repair moment

From the fixed statement, **Refine** opens a two-part ordinary-language correction:

1. **This applies when:** externally consequential work ships.
2. **An exception is:** reversible internal drafts may remain delegated.

CTRL composes the resulting statement below the preserved original:

> Before externally consequential work ships, the human owns final judgement and finish; reversible internal drafts may remain delegated.

Before commitment, the Consequences lens becomes the effect preview at the point of action:

- **Updates now:** Portrait and Connections.
- **Needs your review:** Board brief and shared Brain release.
- **Will not happen:** Delivered work will not be silently rewritten.

The single dominant action is **Apply correction**. After it is used, the new wording replaces the original at the fixed point. Only the changed phrases receive one brief, state-responsive emphasis. In Connections, the old standard-to-aim and voice-to-standard relationships withdraw and their current replacements arrive with revised explanations. The accepted tension-to-aim relationship remains unchanged.

The interface then selects Consequences and shows four terminal receipts: **Portrait rebuilt**, **Connections rebuilt**, **Board brief requires review**, and **Shared release requires review**. **Previous meaning** keeps the superseded version and reason inspectable. Replaying the same action cannot create another change.

## 9. Sparse and dense Brains

For a sparse first-use Brain, the fixed point may stand alone. Sources can still establish why it exists. Connections says **No supported connections yet** and draws no speculative line. Portrait can offer **Add a judgement from a real decision** as a next task, but empty category furniture and completion scoring are absent.

For 120 items and 180 relationships, the interaction does not become a zoomable hairball. Connections opens at an orientation set of at most eighteen eligible current entities. Selecting any entity makes it the new fixed point and shows a neighbourhood containing that focus plus at most twenty-three directly relevant or related entities. Selecting a relationship phrase reveals item-and-evidence detail. Ordering and membership remain deterministic under source reordering.

Long labels wrap and increase the height of the fixed point rather than shrinking type or truncating the judgement. On narrow screens, only one relationship explanation is expanded at a time. The page has one scroll axis and no nested scrolling region.

## 10. Strongest reason it could fail

The concept could feel too narrow. By insisting on one fixed judgement, it may underrepresent the emotional value of seeing the whole Brain alive and may make comparison across several standards slower than a leader expects. If cold users do not understand why this particular judgement was selected, the stable focal point will feel like CTRL's arbitrary assertion about them rather than their own recognisable thinking. The material proof must therefore test whether **Why this is in focus**, explicit task scope, and fast focus switching are enough to preserve both immediacy and ownership without turning the surface into a multi-item dashboard.

## 11. Constraints that make it materially distinct

1. The surface may never present multiple equal panels. One fixed judgement and one active lens must dominate every state.
2. Portrait and Connections may not be separate destinations. Their switch must preserve the same focal object in place, making canonical parity perceptible rather than merely asserted.
3. A visible connection must be a labelled, inspectable, current, evidence-bearing relationship. Proximity, animation, similarity, and layout tethers may not substitute for semantics.
4. Correction may not collapse into an edit box. It must preview affected projections and consequential dependencies before commitment, then show one terminal receipt for every known use.
5. The experience may not open with a chat prompt, memory inventory, score, activity feed, or graph overview. It opens with a specific piece of the user's judgement and asks whether it is still right.
6. Motion is reserved for a lens change or a real state transition such as correction, replacement, dispute, or completed repair. Nothing moves merely to make the Brain appear intelligent.
7. Proposed, disputed, superseded, stale, private, and failed states must change what can be shown and acted upon. They cannot be reduced to decorative badges on otherwise identical content.
