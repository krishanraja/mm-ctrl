## Adversarial method

Every plausible veto was tested in four steps:

1. Identify an exact section or JSON pointer.
2. Describe the unsafe implementation and ask whether it truly conforms to all inherited and amended rules.
3. Decide whether the alleged choice is within the narrow G24.A architecture scope.
4. Apply the frozen gate boundary instead of importing a later schema, runtime, UI or efficacy requirement.

The standard permits a veto only where a conforming implementer can still make a consequential unsafe, contradictory or product-defining choice that G24.A must own. A nonconforming implementation is not evidence of an architecture ambiguity. A real later-gate uncertainty is not evidence that the later gate has already passed.

**Exact locator:** g24-product-system-blueprint-r4.md, Repair 1; g24-product-system-contract-r4.json at /epistemic_eligibility_binding.

**Failure hypothesis:** An implementer labels a model-authored policy as current, supplies a vacuous challenger search boundary, or reuses a result that is not bound to the exact decision requirement, evidence coverage, cutoff and policy version. **Conformance challenge:** A model-authored label, missing or stale reference, inapplicable result, indeterminate result, or result without the four-way challenger binding violates the candidate. The contract assigns source capability, independence, sufficiency, applicability, causality, contradiction and countercase status to trusted evaluation under the existing R1 governance owner. The harder residual question is whether an accepted policy's contents and a declared search boundary are genuinely adequate. That requires exact policy fixtures, authenticity/currentness checks, semantic-oracle tests and runtime evidence. Those belong to G24.B/C. ### 2. Invalid controls could leak through an omitted dimension or an impossible hold envelope

**Exact locator:** g24-product-system-blueprint-r4.md, normative precedence and Repair 3; g24-product-system-contract-r4.json at /normative_precedence and /selector_policy_replacement; inherited g24-product-system-contract-r3.json selector totality and output fields.

**Failure hypothesis:** An invalid value outside the R4 examples produces enrich, ask or session, or a missing accepted frame forces the hold receipt to fabricate a material effect merely to satisfy inherited output fields.

**Conformance challenge:** R4 replaces only the named R3 seams and inherits every other R3 rule. R3 already makes the selector total over missing, stale, ambiguous and contradictory input and gives invalid controlling input hard precedence. R4 strengthens that rule with explicit invalid conditions and dimensions, exactly one output, no actionable derivative, and no approval or delivery entry. If the accepted frame is missing, fabricating a frame-bound material effect would contradict both the canonical binding and the ban on an actionable derivative. Whether a later physical schema represents an inapplicable dependent field as absent, null or a typed not-evaluable value is a schema choice, not an unresolved authority or safety choice at G24.A.

**Disposition:** Rejected as a current veto. Exact hold-envelope typing remains a G24.B/C schema test.

### 3. Valid unresolved evidence could be mistaken for permission to act

**Exact locator:** g24-product-system-contract-r4.json at /selector_policy_replacement/valid_unresolved_evidence and /selector_policy_replacement/provisional; inherited R3 selector and intervention-atom rules.

**Failure hypothesis:** A contradiction is called valid, then enrich, ask or session is treated as a provisional decision or enters approval before it is resolved.

**Conformance challenge:** R4 distinguishes validity of the controlling context from resolution of the evidence. Provisional status has no route standing or authority, may exist only as diagnostic metadata on the hold receipt, and cannot create an intervention, enter approval, change the Brain or bypass invalidation. ### 4. The lifecycle graph could strand or silently reactivate an engagement

**Exact locator:** g24-product-system-blueprint-r4.md, Repair 2; g24-product-system-contract-r4.json at /lifecycle_policy_replacement.

**Failure hypothesis:** Abandoned preparation has no safe exit, an unlisted edge is inferred, commercial state grants permission, reopening revives expired authority, or delayed work continues after pause or close.

**Conformance challenge:** The graph is not a suggestive picture. It declares exactly six states and 13 edges, forbids grouped or implicit edges, requires a universal transition envelope, makes absence of a receipt non-operative, and rejects stale transitions. preparing to closed is the explicit safe exit for abandoned preparation. paused prohibits new decision-shaping work. closing and closed restrict activity to the named close, access, correction, separate Release, retention and deletion duties. closed to preparing starts a new bounded decision and does not revive expired authority. Commercial or elapsed state grants no permission.

Adding an inferred transition, accepting stale compare-and-swap state, or allowing a queued action after invalidation is therefore nonconforming. The architecture owns the graph and capability semantics now. Atomic compare-and-swap behavior, idempotency, late-job cancellation and failure injection are G24.B/C proof.

**Disposition:** Rejected as a current veto. Concurrency and queued-work tests remain later-gate obligations.

## Strongest residual risk

The strongest residual risk is dependency invalidation between a challenger result and a pending Release projection.

**Exact locator:** g24-product-system-contract-r4.json at /epistemic_eligibility_binding/invalidation_triggers and /release_non_inference/pending_projection_invalidation_triggers.

The first pointer invalidates selector outputs when either the epistemic policy version or independent challenger result version changes. The second pointer expressly names epistemic-policy changes and included source or Brain version changes for pending projections, but does not separately enumerate an independent-challenger-result version change.

This is not sustained as a current veto. The Release projection is owned by R1, binds canonical source and Brain versions, and remains subject to inherited dependency traversal. A pending projection that actually depends on an invalidated selector result cannot use that result and conform. A projection with no such dependency should not be invalidated merely because an unrelated decision's challenger result changed.
