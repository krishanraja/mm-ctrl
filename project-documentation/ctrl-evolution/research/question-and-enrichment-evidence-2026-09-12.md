## Evidence-supported working principles

1. Design backwards from the decision. A question earns interruption only if its answer can change a named route, evidence requirement, human boundary, stop condition, learning proposal or session agenda.
2. Prefer the easiest truthful source. Use accepted Brain context, authorised work, primary public evidence and observed behaviour before asking someone to restate what can already be known.
3. Ask about a recent bounded incident before a broad attitude or future intention when observed behaviour is the construct that matters.
4. Match the answer control to the information required. Use numeric entry for quantities, a bounded recall choice for behaviour, a fully labelled scale for degree, forced choice for a trade-off, best and worst selection for a long priority set, and one focused open response for a reason.
5. Keep every visible question concrete, single-purpose and answerable. Its wording, options, optional depth and downstream effect form one contract.
6. Avoid mobile grids, long rankings, dropdown dependence, forced answers and mandatory essays. Use one column, large tap targets, voice where a real example is needed and an honest escape route where the person may not know or wish to answer.
7. Separate constructs that people often collapse: stated preference from revealed behaviour, intent from commitment, importance from trade-off, personal judgement from perceived norm, and memory from estimation.
8. Sensitive questions need a truthful reason, a private mode, a legitimate refusal path and careful timing. A privacy promise must describe the actual data path.
9. Ordering can change answers. The system must record why a question appears now and avoid priming the leader with CTRL's recommendation before capturing their provisional view.
10. Completion is a constraint, not the goal. A short interaction can still be wasteful; a longer live conversation can be justified when it materially advances a consequential decision.

## Product-specific synthesis

The old repository theory said that exhaustive computation belongs underneath a calm surface. That principle survives. CTRL may inspect many evidence gaps, but the leader sees only the smallest intervention that has earned attention.

The historical intake work also demonstrated that recognition, pre-filled choices, optional enrichment and visible payoff reduce effort. What does not survive is the idea that a fixed cascade, profile-completion journey or generic questionnaire is the product. G24 needs a decision-value planner that can choose among research, observation, a tap, voice, a request to Krish or a prepared live session.

## Hypotheses that still require observed testing

- A one-question mobile moment will feel simpler without concealing meaningful depth.
- A prepared choice will outperform an open prompt for time-poor leaders when the choices are genuinely specific and complete.
- Voice will produce better critical incidents than typing without increasing perceived burden.
- A visible consequence after answering will improve trust and willingness to return.
- A ranked operator session opportunity will help Krish intervene at the right moment without creating another noisy task queue.
- Progress indicators may help or harm depending on the journey. They are not a default.

These hypotheses require cognitive interviews, actual-device testing and an assisted pilot. Passing repository checks cannot establish them.
