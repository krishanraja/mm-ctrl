## Decision

Krish approves the exact frozen R75 predicate-authority architecture as the governing basis for a separate local machine-contract design and verification gate.

The locked rule is:

> CTRL may treat a required fact or judgement as established only when it is supplied by the canonical system or named human who owns that exact meaning, inside a complete, current and purpose-bound evidence picture. An LLM may locate, compare, challenge and explain evidence, but it cannot create truth, satisfy a precondition or grant transition authority.

## Why this route won

- It keeps mechanical truth, human judgement and derived facts distinct.
- It prevents a fluent model answer from becoming authority merely because it sounds plausible.
- It protects one canonical Brain from silent promotion, stale facts and widened meaning.
- It makes correction, revocation and dependency repair first-class rather than retrospective cleanup.
- It preserves a radically simple customer experience: the system handles mechanical gaps and asks at most one human-owned consequential question.
- It permits external canonical facts only when their real owner can prove freshness at the decision boundary.

## Material alternatives rejected

- **Generic signed satisfaction assertions:** rejected because a signed `true` can launder missing meaning, incomplete evidence or stale authority.
- **LLM interpretation of prose as sufficient evidence:** rejected because confidence and fluency are not authority.
- **One database pretending to own every kind of truth:** rejected because normative human judgement and external canonical facts have different legitimate owners.
- **Holding the decision open:** rejected because R75 passed all seven roles and the remaining uncertainty belongs in the machine-contract proof, not the architecture choice.

## Strongest counterpoint

This architecture is materially more demanding than a conventional LLM wrapper. Cross-system freshness, revocable authority, complete-set proofs and self-healing dependency traversal create implementation complexity. Krish accepts that cost because false authority inside a consequential decision system is a blocking credibility failure, while hidden technical complexity is additive when it protects a simple human experience.

## Authority granted
